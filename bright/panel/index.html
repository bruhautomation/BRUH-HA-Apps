🧪 Test mode — beats & drops

Plays an analyzed track on a calibrated speaker while every ticked bulb pulses on the beat, and the whole selection goes dark-then-full-blast on every drop the analyzer heard — no choreography, just the sync chain and the drop detection end to end. If the pulses ride the beat and the flashes land on the drops, shows will too. Pulses late or early everywhere? Nudge the calibration. Flashes landing where the music does nothing? The room

Tap a light to select it, then drag it where it is in the room (left ↔ right matters most — chases and sweeps travel across x). The selected light can be re-roled, put in a zone, or removed right here. Add bulbs one at a time from the picker below — with a role and a zone — or drop every discovered one on the floor at once and sort them out afterwards.

Roles and zones are two different questions. A role is what a light IS — a candle stays warm and low and never strobes, a lamp carries the beat, a strip carries motion, a party light is a switch. A zone is just a name you give a group, usually a room: type the same word on several lights and they become one. Nothing needs a zone, and there is no list to maintain — a zone exists exactly as long as a light is in it. What zones buy you is being able to say "the kitchen" in an effect, or to send a chase travelling zone by zone through the house rather than straight across the floor plan.

No microphone? Tap along instead

      
Plays the same clicks; you tap the big button on
        each one. Your reaction time rides along (~100ms), so prefer the
        microphone when you can.

      
      
      

    


    

      
Or type the delay in

      
A show will not start without a calibration
        profile, so one speaker that refuses to play the click track takes
        the whole add-on with it. This writes a profile you typed — stored
        as manual, because it was never measured. 0 is right for a
        wired speaker; around 2000 for AirPlay; measure properly when you
        can.
