Light Map

Place each light, give it a role and a spot in the house. The director choreographs from this map: candles glow and never strobe, lamps and downlights carry the beat, strips carry sweeps, and party lights and lasers are saved for the moments that earn them.

The room

Tap a light to select it, then drag it where it is in the room (left ↔ right matters most — chases and sweeps travel across x). The selected light can be re-roled, put in a zone, or removed right here. Add bulbs one at a time from the picker below — with a role and a zone — or drop every discovered one on the floor at once and sort them out afterwards.

Roles and zones are two different questions. A role is what a light IS — a candle stays warm and low and never strobes, a lamp carries the beat, a strip carries motion, a party light is a switch. A zone is just a name you give a group, usually a room: type the same word on several lights and they become one. Nothing needs a zone, and there is no list to maintain — a zone exists exactly as long as a light is in it. What zones buy you is being able to say "the kitchen" in an effect, or to send a chase travelling zone by zone through the house rather than straight across the floor plan.

Effects

An effect is a thing some of your lights do for a stretch of music: a chase across the room, a build into the drop, a strobe on the last four bars. Pick the lights it owns — everything you do not pick is left exactly as it was — set it up against a tempo, watch it, then save it or drop it into a show.

Library

Your music, analyzed ahead of time: tempo and the beat grid, song sections and drops, and time-synced lyrics where they exist. Analysis is the slow part of a show, done once per track — the show itself compiles from this. Add more folders with the add-on's Extra music folders option; each one is scanned all the way down, so subfolders need no entry of their own.

Or type the delay in

      
A show will not start without a calibration
        profile, so one speaker that refuses to play the click track takes
        the whole add-on with it. This writes a profile you typed — stored
        as manual, because it was never measured. 0 is right for a
        wired speaker; around 2000 for AirPlay; measure properly when you
        can.

Party

    
Every analyzed track in the folder, shuffled, each with
      its own show — the next one choreographs itself while the current one
      plays. Save the whole arrangement as a named party and an automation,
      a dashboard button or a voice command can ask for it by name:
      bright.start_party. Stopping restores every light to how
      it was, unless the party names a scene to call instead — which is what
      you want when the show was the evening.
