# brAIn: house checks: history moved out of docs/notes/checks.md

What each rule in `docs/notes/checks.md` cost to learn. None of it is needed to follow the rules; it is here for the next person who wonders why a floor exists. Pointers to tests under `tests/` name files that were removed in November 2026 with the old suite; the rules they enforced still hold.

## How the scorecard caught the checks that fired on the wrong things

- **`dev.frozen` and `state_class: measurement`.** The scorecard read 0 confirmed against 6 marked Wrong, with ten more filed in the same pass: the Findings tab saying the rule is wrong about this house, `dev.implausible`'s failure one check over. `duration` was missed by the first cut; it arrived the way the ambient-temperature gap did, as a real house, a real row and a sensor working as intended. The test named it beside `current` on purpose. Estimates published under a real meter's class were a second source of false rows.
- **Stuck reading beside a flat battery.** `dev.frozen` was Wrong 6 of 6 ("it's still reading, probably low battery", "its battery is dead and I do not plan to replace it") and `dev.unavailable` 4 of 5.
- **Printer bed at 65 °C and nozzle at 220 °C.** A working 3D printer had four rows saying its readings were impossible: 0 confirmed against 2 marked Wrong, the exact failure the catalog opens by naming (a check that fires on a healthy house is the one a person learns to ignore first).
- **Printer heaters in `base.unusual`.** It compared a nozzle and a bed with an hour-of-the-week median whatever the printer was doing, and BRUH Bambu Studio's `bruh_bambu` sensors were not on the printer list: 0 confirmed against 4 Wrong, two of them saying "only unusual if hot while the printer is idle". Tests: `tests/test_running_machine_readings.py`.
- **3.8.6 read the providing integration first and was still answered Wrong** about a nozzle, a bed and a processor: the platform answered only when it was a printer's or a computer's own, and otherwise `world_model`'s reading of the ENTITY ran ahead of the device's evidence, so a bed published by a bridge on a printer maker's device was room air whenever the model said so; a setpoint ("nozzle target temperature") was judged as a reading by `dev.implausible` while `base.unusual` already stood it down.
- **A computer's processor temperature was filed as impossible a fourth time** because nothing it carried was on a list: an integration spelled `system_monitor` against the table's `systemmonitor`, a model that read "temperature" as room air, a sensor named for the box rather than the chip.
- **"Normal while the printer is printing. Only unusual if hot while the printer is idle"** was stored as `exception:base.unusual` with no condition, and the check could never raise either sensor again. "Normal while the dishwasher is running" (no "only") had the same fate. Tests: `tests/test_condition_without_only.py`.
- **A probe swinging daily from a room's reading to −150 °F** has a possible hourly mean, so the nightly pass kept those hours, built the recent level out of them, and `base.unusual`'s drift guard excused it six times as the season or the weather while `dev.implausible` filed it as faulty. `gave_up` used to write `cap` for every stand-down.
- **The stand-down while a status says working was half the answer.** The band mixed print hours with idle ones, so an idle bed at room temperature in an hour the printer usually prints was "far below" a usual of 45 °C, and a status published as Home Assistant's `running` binary sensor was never read, so a print was a fault. Tests: `tests/test_machine_state_bands.py`.
- **"That's normal for this sensor"** on `base.unusual` or `dev.implausible` used to be a rule for ever; the explained range replaced that. Tests: `tests/test_explained_reading_range.py`.
- **`base.unusual` under the cap.** 23, 17 and 17 rows against a limit of 4 left every real anomaly unchecked, and three rooms' humidity falling in one pass while the outdoor air was dry filed three cards on a house the same check had called the season that morning.
- **`not_a_house_reading`.** A forecast, a print job and a runtime counter beside one real fault took the real fault down with them past `MAX_ROWS`. A washer's plug at 178 W against a usual 8 W while its status read "spinning" was filed every wash, because the appliance store had no profile for it; a washer whose plug was named for nothing was cut behind an inverter's circuits and never profiled. Tests: `tests/test_not_a_house_reading.py`, `tests/test_cycling_machine_reading.py`.
- **Print stopped by the printer.** Two stopped prints in a day (paused at 10 % by the printer's own failure detection with the bed still hot, and one failed that afternoon) produced nothing, because no check read a printer's status for anything but a finished job.
- **Where a device is.** Checks appended `where()` after a finished sentence ("…its normal variation. in the Basement.", "10% as of 8 Oct in the Hall"), and a sensor named for one room but assigned to another came out as the two joined. Tests: `tests/test_finding_area_sentence.py`.
- **A phone feature switched off.** `dev.unavailable` was answered Wrong 4 of 4 (arrived beside 2.18.3's stand-downs); the device row used to re-file a device somebody had answered when a restart re-stamped a sibling into the lead.
- **`auto.never_fired` on safety automations.** It read "state-triggered, nothing in 30 days" as dead, which is what a healthy smoke, leak, CO or gas response looks like, including brAIn's own playbooks: a month after somebody accepted the smoke playbook, brAIn offered to remove it. The alarm-about-a-fault rule came from 0 confirmed against 2 Wrong. The test's playbooks are built by the real `playbooks.build` and written by the real `entry_for`.
- **`auto.conflict` sequences.** Pairs that undid each other in both directions minutes apart, each answering its own trigger ("off at eleven" beside "motion on"), were most of what it filed: 3 of 4 marked Wrong. Six Wrongs in a day about one automation came from writing and asking only the first-sorted rule of a pair. 5.3.6 read `owner_explained` under any check for the fought-over entity, so a reason on a battery row of the same device quietened a real fight and a reason on the conflict itself was not read.
- **Wrong answers that did not hold.** `dev.unavailable` and `dev.frozen` kept re-raising after Wrong answers (a Wrong whose reason wrote no rule was one short of `WRONG_LIMIT`, and the ledger answer was read only under the entity the row led with); `auto.never_fired` filed an automation again after every earlier report about it was marked Wrong; `chore.dehumidifier` already asked `shadow.not_due` while `chore.waiting` did not, so a dehumidifier on a plug named like a dryer was "finished and waiting" while its humidity rule read under its set point. Issues #810, #811, #812, #813, #826, #848, #852, #863, #579, #768, #772, #864, #865, #862, #827, #849, #828, #697, #702, #707, #675, #770, #877, #440. Tests: `tests/test_rule_held_by_its_record.py`, `tests/test_answered_once_never_snoozed.py`, `tests/test_chore_waiting_reads_its_rule.py`.
- **Eight reports with one cause** (the household-explanation reader): each check read only its own Wrongs, under its own entity, and only while a rule's explained range or the device's current stretch held.
- **Faint Bluetooth.** The check decided on signal strength alone and told the owner that a device heard at a very weak signal, reporting well over a thousand times a day at its usual pace, had readings that "arrive late or not at all"; a device under the line on one pass was filed and cleared on the next; a Done was filed again as new on the next pass. Tests: `tests/test_bluetooth_proxy_offline_first.py`.
- **`auto.overridden` first cut.** `_band` searched four-hour windows and took whichever start it tried first, so fifteen overrides all at 08:10 came back as "between 05:00 and 09:00". The counted-without-denominator version reported three undos of three runs and of three hundred identically. Tests: `tests/test_overridden_press.py`, `tests/test_evening_lock_words.py`, `tests/test_rule_record_notice.py`.

## Checks and snapshot history

- **History cut-offs.** A card said a pump "had not cycled in 58+ hours" off one power sensor at 0 W while another sensor on it showed the cycle. Shared seconds did not catch a restart whose integrations came up over several minutes, and a network counter the recorder excludes still has rows from before the exclude: four of twelve probed entities were called cut on a house whose recorder was fine, and `dev.frozen` reported "could not run" (#702, #707). `history_cutoffs` called every restart-re-stamped entity cut, standing `dev.frozen` down while `sys.history_incomplete` reported a recorder missing rows it was not. Tests: `tests/test_history_incomplete_restart.py`.
- **`sys.update_pending`.** brAIn asked after backups, add-ons, the host and Core and never what was waiting, so the one question a person opens Home Assistant to answer was the one brAIn could not. It would otherwise have been six cards to dismiss one at a time about one afternoon's work. `score_checks` and `replay.render` both said "skipped": the collision was invisible for as long as no checks entry had ever skipped anything, and the first skip on a frozen house turned both into "skipped — {...}" with nothing scored and recall 0%. The shadow set stopped being empty with this check, so the test pins its contents rather than counting them.
- **Traces.** The checks looked runs up by entity id and read `script_execution` at the top level of a row that nests it, so on a real install they found nothing and the fixture wrote the code's own guess down (keyed by entity id, flat rows). The fixture rows are `as_short_dict` (Core `homeassistant/components/trace/models.py`); `tests/test_live_traces.py` drove the real `collect()` against a fake Core speaking the WebSocket protocol. A live read sees the daytime `failed_conditions` runs the shutdown file mostly did not.
- **Purpose-specific triggers.** `shadow.check_replayable` refused every one as "the recorder does not keep what it fires on", so every such automation lost its replay, its trial and its condition card's evidence. The `for:` hold bug: it ended at the NEXT recorded row whatever it said.
- **Node status sensor.** Found by `entity_id.endswith("_node_status")`, so a node dead for days was never reported and its frozen 0% battery was filed as a serious low battery. A forecast resting on 1% had a MAD of zero, so the 2% floor made its usual step "200 times its normal variation".
- **Second set of checks.** Written as 1.29's follow-up: all 1.29 checks read Core, and the ones that matter most read around it.
- **Network checks.** The owner's ask; one Wi-Fi switch was three open rows (a check's on its switch, a card's on its UniFi tracker, a third inside a four-device card) and the UniFi integration losing its controller was two (#440): every fold keyed on an ENTITY. The owner got a card per device from five checks, most about failures that had passed (`tests/test_connectivity_card.py`). A dead battery node hid for a year behind `last_changed`.
- **`integ.log_flood`.** A hundred errors in the twenty minutes round a restart cleared the floor and became a card nobody could act on by the time it was read (`tests/test_log_flood_transient.py`).
- **`auto.conflict` and `find_overrides`.** The override rules named the conflict check and deferred it; the evening check and the shadow store likewise were written as one feature each.
- **Shadow store.** Its five "reaches nobody" claims are five tests, each verified by filing the same row to the real store and watching that surface light up.
- **Doors.** The first `spread_interval` charged the span to the hour it started in, so a rarely-changing entity reported almost every hour as unwatched.
- **Companion second voice.** Owner-asked (#675): one voice says "I can't" while the other does it.
- **Frost.** Written against a near-freezing night; kept apart from `climate.freeze`.
