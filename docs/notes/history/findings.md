# brAIn: the findings store: history moved out of docs/notes/findings.md

What each rule in `docs/notes/findings.md` cost to learn. None of it is needed to follow the rules; it is here for the next person who wonders why a fold, a hold or a word rule exists. Pointers to tests under `tests/` name files that were removed in November 2026 with the old suite; the rules they enforced still hold.

## What a finding is

- **One list of decisions.** Open questions used to be answerable in three places — inline under every insight card, in the Memory tab's "Waiting on you", and counted by neither badge — so answering one left the other two rendering an open question, and the Memory badge counted work the Findings badge didn't. What is left on the Memory tab is a queue that files itself and a document to read.

## Ending a finding

- **"Check again".** A check can file something true of a moment that has since passed — a device unavailable while its hub rebooted, a reading implausible while a printer was hot, an add-on stopped mid-update. Every other button makes a person claim the problem is fixed or was never real; the scheduled pass would clear it in up to `checks_interval_hours`, which on a list you are looking at now may as well be never. Clearing only the row that was pressed would leave the others to be pressed one at a time about a thing the same look already proved is over.
- **Why Wrong takes a reason.** "Ignore" described what happened to the row; what people mean is almost always "you have misread my house" — the sensor is not stuck, it is a contact on a cupboard nobody opens. The consolidator is told the report is not a fact; deliberately a judgement call, because nobody phrases a correction carefully, and a button that wrote whatever was typed into `memory.md` would file the thing being denied. Explaining a Wrong to Claude in the chat reaches that conversation; the note reaches every future one.
- **The archive.** Rendering a per-status pile beside a list that is meant to empty invited people to treat it as the record when memory already is. **That press lived only in this file for four releases**: the "Let brAIn raise it again" route had no caller in the panel, the CLI or the integration, because the rule as written forbade any view of the ledger at all. So the rule was narrowed rather than dropped; a filter over the list could only ever be empty, because settling deletes the row, and `open` counts work waiting on a person, which an answered thing by definition is not.
- **The ledger lapse.** A fixed problem that came back was suppressed for the life of the install, because the ledger treated "I've fixed it" like Wrong.
- **A hand fix checked by its check.** Nothing ended a person's `fixed` while the check went on reporting the problem, so a fix that did not hold was deduped for ever.

## To Do

- **The fourth ending.** The commonest honest answer to a finding had nowhere to go: not "I fixed it", not "you have this wrong", but yes, it is real and I will do it. So a flat battery sat there as an open question for as long as it took to get round to, the badge counted it every day, and a list of decisions filled up with chores nobody could clear without lying about one of them.
- **Why `accepted` counts as confirmed.** Counting it as nothing until the chore is finished would make a producer look worse the more of its reports people took seriously and had not got round to. Keying `remember_answer` on a second derivation from a copy of the text works right up until the two disagree.
- **The other To Do rules.** The whole point of the move is that the row goes away, so a pointer dangles by construction. Finished chores are kept because forgetting what you did this week means being unable to answer "did I already do that"; *Put it back* leaving the memory line is the same admission the undo token's five-minute life makes. Making room at the cap would mean dropping something somebody else put there. A finding that vanished into a list that would not take it is the one outcome with no way back, which is why the item is created first.
- **Chores in the queue.** The read-model listed all four stores, and a finding moved to To Do came straight back beside the real findings as a card with no way onto the list.
- **Every finding says what To Do will say.** An item on To Do read as the finding's claim ("Battery at 9%") or the check's name; Morph's To Do needs the errand ("Replace the battery in the hallway motion sensor."). `derive_todo_text` cut the fix's first "errand" clause and bound pronouns to the name, and three rounds of rules for statements, conditionals, labels and pronouns still produced "Look at the Hall sensor: it battery low." and "Hall sensor: 1.", which is why nothing is derived from free text (#879).

## Folding and dedupe

- **The subject fold.** A model-written producer rewords every pass, so ten cards about one sensor across three weeks was the dedupe working exactly as written.
- **The cross-producer fold.** Two insight cards reading one sensor each filed their own wording of one diagnosis, six rows in ten days, because the fold was keyed on the source. `test_subject_fold`'s different-producer case holds the Resident boundary.
- **One cause, one row.** A lock's weak Bluetooth link and the same lock's "stopped reporting its battery" were two rows with two steps, and nothing said the first explains the second.
- **A model's link (#792, withdrawn after two reviews).** A `cause` key and a look's or investigation's `same_as` folded through `_fold_devices` hid real problems twice: first a leak sensor, a smoke alarm and a lock under an unrelated pending question, then — after a strict floor on entity class and name — a row with no entity ("water is leaking under the kitchen sink", whose text `_take_in` keeps only with an entity id) and a siren, a camera and a sump switch.
- **Read against the house (#722).** "The hall plug is unplugged and 6 scenes still set it" stayed up after the plug came back: "unplugged" was not a drop word and the sweep waits `RETURN_SETTLE_S`.
- **Gone is not down (#789).** "Hall plug is unplugged and 2 scenes still set it" was marked Wrong because the plug was no longer in Home Assistant at all — `holds_now` only knew states, and "not heard from proves nothing".

## Muting and holding a rule

- **The mute.** The scorecard existed to say a producer was wrong about a house — `forecast.decline` 0 confirmed against 3 marked Wrong — and the only answer the tab had was Wrong one row at a time. Filing muted rows and hiding them would have put a mute into `LIVE_STATUSES`, the mirror, the badge and every dedupe. The group's `Device check` title, three times over, is three things nobody can tell apart. The first cut of the mute route spread the payload *after* the flag and returned the list under the flag's name.
- **Advice and the read-only tools.** A sentence naming the hub and the automation does not fit in a button; a specific instruction and a generic one read alike until you know which looked at your house. The Findings tab was reachable by nothing a run could call, so "what needs attention" was answered by guessing and "is brAIn OK" not at all.
- **A notification opens the panel.** A tap landed on Home Assistant's front page with the row three taps away; a made-up path opens the wrong add-on or nothing.
- **The mute offer.** `auto.conflict` 1 against 4 Wrong and `forecast.decline` 0/3 kept filing and nothing offered to stop them.
- **One name.** The question asked "Stop raising 'Device check'?" because the pass read the raw scorecard, whose title was the GROUP a check files under — a word naming a dozen rules, the useful battery rule among them — while the fault list and the card said the catalog's own title. "The scorecard" is a word nothing on screen shows (#439). A row filed under an automation the exception never named does not involve the one it was recorded under.
- **Three Wrongs deep.** The record line read to the first look (`rule_record.qualifies`, two Wrongs) and the mute question (four answers) left rules at 0 of 6, 0 of 4, 0 of 3 and 0 of 2 confirmed filing, because each Wrong was per entity or per pair, the next automation, forecast or card a person made was news again, and a row no look answered for surfaced unjudged. A check's row carries none of the signal's flags, which is why `never_held` matches protection on the row's own entity.
- **Held by its share (#811, #812, #826).** A streak ends at any agreement, so `dev.unavailable` and `auto.conflict` at four Wrong in five, the one agreement among the latest answers, were never held. Before the `record` trail row, only the row's own History entry said why it was held.

## Answers about one thing

- **Stop watching.** An `exception:*` rule on an entity already stood down every check on it and, through `_device_rule`, on its device; only a reason the `correct` job read as "anything about it" could write one, so there was no press for it.
- **Stop raising (#1079).** "don't remind me", "stop reminding me" and "you don't need to remind me" became holds that lifted when the report changed, so the battery, the empty Matter setup and the weak router came back.
- **An answer reaching every producer (#1263, #1264, #1315, #1323).** Four ways an owner's answer escaped:
  - **The overnight check wrote no rule**: "not in use" on its card settled one wording and told no check, so the Zigbee-link and health checks went on raising the device.
  - **The overnight check held back log lines only**: its Zigbee and Z-Wave records about a device set aside went to the run as mesh trouble.
  - **"Knob 1 is not in use, remove it from automations" was not read as the clean-up request** because `asks_cleanup` took the instruction only alone.
  - **A hold that lifted and was answered again was held again, for ever**: a second "don't remind me" after a hold lifted made another hold, never a stand-down.

## Corrections, snoozes and holds

- **A rule's identity.** Two checks marked Wrong on one sensor wrote the same text under the same subject, hashed to one id, and the second silently replaced the first: one check stood down, the other came back on the next pass with nothing on screen to say why. "That is normal here" under no report is a sentence no run can use, which is why the row carries `about`.
- **Scope.** "It's always like that in winter" is not "never again", and "none of the garage plugs matter" is a room. A room scope written in March cannot know a leak sensor was fitted there in May, which is why the floor is also read at READ time.
- **The snooze.** A dashboard clean-up only offered removal, and the household's "I need to fix this light" was stored as a general correction — nothing stayed quiet for the repair and the suggestion came back while the light was still out. Examples read as temporary: "I'll plug it back in soon"; as permanent: "do not plan to replace it".
- **The hold (#602, #694, #698).** 4.0.15 made the words ("stop reminding me", "it's on my to do list", "I am aware, don't bring this up again") a 30-day snooze (#534), and the house's evidence was that a period is the wrong ending for a static fact: an empty Matter setup, a short camera retention or a weak router is the same fact next month, so it came back unchanged; and "stop bringing up …", "stop telling me", "quit flagging" were not read at all. A battery 20% → 5% or a room 14 °C → 9 °C within one severity is not the report the answer was about, a wobble is; three days of absence because one pass that missed it is not the fact ending, so a flapping router is still the same fact. Not deferrals: "they don't notify me when the door opens", "don't tell me it's offline, it's reporting wrong", "I'm aware, fix it now", "don't tell me what to do", "I'm aware, the pump is leaking", "I'm already aware, it's the water heater leaking". A hold would keep a wrong report on the books until the wrong sensor changed, which is why a request that also says the report is wrong stays a Wrong.
- **The interpreter.** Each of the four boxes used to decide what words meant by itself: the ask bar by four regexes on the first word, a Reply was always a question for the Resident, the Repairs box was always a reason for Wrong, the service was always an automation.

## The decision trail

- **Why brAIn said nothing.** Every surface reported what brAIn did; none could answer "why didn't you tell me". A paused house holds the same batch every minute, hence `MERGE_S`.
- **Standing decisions fold.** Each checks pass made the same "you said you had fixed it" about the same row, ten lines a day.

## The words on a finding

- **Plain prose.** Cards told a homeowner to "run brain.delete_orphaned_devices (it is a dry run unless you tell it otherwise)", listed orphaned devices with their MAC in brackets and named a helper as "Guest mode (input_select.guest_mode)": developer vocabulary.
- **What was measured (#583, #725, #726, #735, #737).** A first attempt read prose broadly and broke real rows: it held a wet leak sensor because its reason said "can wait", dropped "replace the battery" under "the battery was fine yesterday, but it is at 4% now", deleted "exclude the entities that write most often", trimmed the only step off leak and smoke cards, and printed counts nobody kept ("usually it logs this a handful of times"). A version that dropped a sentence whose battery or signal the detail called fine also took "shut off the water at the main" and "unlock the door by hand" with it.
- **No developer string (#774, #791).** `technical` used to write "an internal name" where a snake_case code name had been ("the an internal name event"): the sentence broke and nobody could tell which thing was meant. Four gaps let paths, `tts.…`/`sensor.…` ids, hex codes and builds through #563's rule: `entities` leaves an id with no name on purpose and a service id is in no names map; a look's reason (`files=False`) kept paths and line numbers with its file names; a build with no version in front ("build 24C101") was not read; and a stored row was never read again. Duration (#771): two attempts at reading which duration was the row's fact from the words both changed meaning ("ran for 20 minutes, a normal cycle" became "ran for 3 days"; "offline for 14 hours, then back for 2 hours" became 3 days twice).
- **A check's row (#1205).** The rule that a check's words are never touched let what a device or a log put in them (a setting's key, a file name, a path) reach the card.
- **Insight cards (#1313).** 5.5.2's sweep reached every finding but no card, so a card summary still quoted a path, a `tts.` id, a voice id and a dated build to the household (and to the design review, which reads `_insights_listing`). "Z-Stack 20190101." was not read as a build, and "The file /config/x.md says" read "The file a configuration file says".
- **Voice ids and dated builds (#1133, #1084, #1086).** The display pass missed "voice en_US-kristin-medium" and "Z-Stack 20210708", and what it took out of a sentence was dropped rather than kept. FINDING_VOICE was only in the Resident's prompts, while the overnight check that writes the mesh and firmware rows never had it. "Reserve its address on the router" was a step with its value missing; an IPv6 had already reached the design review before the scrub covered it.
- **Shapes, not tables.** `zwave_js.refresh_value`, `brain.delete_orphaned_devices`, `recorder.purge` and `/dev/ttyUSB0` reached the household because the id rule was a table of entity domains and the path rule a list of first folders. The mirror read `shown_words` without `shown_fix`, so Home Assistant showed steps the card did not ("Nothing is needed.", a path).
- **Several things (#437).** A dashboard row ended "the scene “…”, the select…" and an automation's "and 7 more.…": an inline list of long names ran past the store's `MAX_DETAIL` cap (whose sentence cut ends `.…`) and the card's three lines, and the rest of the list was nowhere.
- **The overdue figure (#1201).** The question carried "19,766 miles" (on a 1,000-mile reminder), so a Wrong settled that day's words and tomorrow's "19,812" was a new row, and it said "A reminder", so one Wrong would have been an answer about every reminder. **#1325 came back on 5.5.2 because both readers were word shapes over the row's prose:** `misread_reminder` needed the figure AND an interval written out, and `odometer_counter` (#827's drop) had been narrowed back to "N miles overdue" alone, so "overdue by N miles", "due N miles ago" or a reading of "-N mi" with no interval beside it was neither asked nor dropped and filed as a warning; and a Wrong stood down only the producer that asked.

## Counts and the scorecard

- **One source for every count.** The walkthrough found every number disagreeing with another: a badge of 2 over a list of 4 (the Findings badge was painted from `/api/status` on one poll and from `/api/findings`' own sum on the next), HA's To-do holding 5, facts 99 in HA (the memory change log) against 755 in the panel (the facts store), a brief saying 677 of 721 runs failed (every failed journal row over every journal line) beside a Diagnostics count of 224 ok, and the health sensor `ok` while Diagnostics said degraded.
- **The scorecard.** `_remember_settled` recorded the ending and not who raised it, so nothing could add the labels up. "1 of 1" is an anecdote, hence `SCORE_MIN_ENDINGS`.
- **Release on every ending** (self-learning plan 1.5). "Worse since 4.x.y" was prose because the ledger had no release on it; counting an old answer under the running release would credit the new version with answers given to an old one.
