# brAIn: insight cards, reports and the brief: history moved out of docs/notes/insight-cards.md

What each rule in `docs/notes/insight-cards.md` cost to learn. None of it is needed to follow the rules; it is here for the next person who wonders why a fold, a verdict rule or a gate exists. Pointers to tests under `tests/` name files that were removed in November 2026 with the old suite; the rules they enforced still hold. Issue numbers are the development loop's reports from houses.

## Generation and refresh

- **A refresh gate fingerprints what the run reads, never when a store was written.** The `changed` mode was a 24-hour timer with extra steps: the stores' parts hashed build stamps and mtimes that move every night whether or not a number did, memory hashed the file's mtime (moved by every consolidation), and the findings part included the card's own rows, so a card filing a finding scheduled its own regeneration. `CATEGORY_STORES` was held equal to the shipped category ids by a test, written after two categories that no longer existed were found still in it.
- **A card fetches what it needs; it is not handed the house.** The one-shot path never could afford history, which is why asked questions were always answered from a single instant.
- **A row in the bundle is 500 rows in the prompt, so its encoding is a cost.** The absolute `lc` form also made the model diff it against `meta.now` to get to staleness. An agentic loop is what the docs already called the most expensive setting in the add-on.
- **Reports shows only cards whose run read the house, decided from what the run did** (#1464).
- **A question that is not about the house is answered in Ask, never on Reports** (#1083, #1135). `read_nothing` held only on the search path, and only for runs whose transcript was found: the snapshot path (a fallback, or `gather_mode` snapshot) was told by `_framing` to "answer briefly in the summary" when the question was not about the house, so "No home data needed for this reply" was stored as a card; and the ask bar's own `chat` route was queued as a card. Reading empty fields as "not about the house" was tried and threw real answers away.
- **"Custom" is not a producer's name.** An asked card filed its findings under its synthetic category's title, so the Findings tab and the scorecard said *Custom* with nothing tying the row to its card.

## What a run costs

- **A run that costs something has to say so, on the thing it cost it for.** The numbers came back in every result envelope, were used to add to a total, and were rendered nowhere. The only visible effect was the usage pill moving, attributable to nothing, which is how "insights use a lot" becomes a suspicion rather than a reading. The run ledger had recorded an id per run since the budget existed and nothing ever read it back. There were once four surfaces, the card's foot carrying `meta.cost` among them; House › Reports cut the foot to one age, and `meta.cost` is still stored on the card but drawn nowhere. `positionChipPop`'s scroll came because the popover held two rows and a sentence when it was written, and a list whose last rows are under the bottom of the screen is the tooltip bug sideways.

## The card contract

- **The title says what was counted, and brAIn's own limits are not news.** "evenings eased from 14 openings to 5" never said openings of what, a kWh in a title named no appliance while the summary led with another figure, and a card told the household the recorder kept only seven nights. The old "say so in the summary" on thin data went with it. The contract was held under `test_model_plan`'s 9100-character ceiling, so the room came from cut examples (the OfficeNet phone, a hypothesis's afterlife), never from a rule.
- **Every figure in a card's title is in its body, with the same unit.** Titles said "0 kWh over the last 8 days" over "all 11 days", "no cooling at 73 °F" over a body that "passed 65 °F", and "flipped from 43 to clear". `tests/test_card_title_figures.py`.
- **A card carries no note about brAIn's own run, and "worth a look" carries a reason or a step** (#1203).
- **A stored card is read by the rules a new one is, and a limit stays only where it changes what to do** (#1311, #1328, #1298). 5.5.2 dropped a stepless "worth a look" and the run notes only in `review`, so a card kept by a hold or stored before the rule still showed both for days. A sentence opening "brAIn …" was never split from the one before it (the split wanted a capital), so its run notes and gaps were read as content. The design review named folded cards by their headline (the blind spot that folded them).
- **A place is its name, never its coordinates.** A finished game build's card was four tiles of coordinates and block lists.
- **A card comes only from an analysis of the house.** A search run that answered "no data needed" without reading anything used to be stored as a card.

## Verdicts and the quiet line

- **A card carries ONE verdict, as a chip, and its prose never says it again.** A house's cards wrote their judgement six ways into the summary ("Verdict: normal.", "Needs action.", "Nothing to fix.", "This needs one change.", "Neither unit is broken."), so the one thing a dashboard is scanned for was a sentence to read, worded differently on every card. `tests/test_card_one_verdict.py`, `measure-reports`.
- **A card's opening verdict is read against its own evidence.** `tests/test_card_verdict_post_check.py`.
- **A "worth a look" its own card contradicts is not the card's verdict, and a tile's "now" ages.** A house's top card wore "Worth a look" over text that found nothing abnormal (cooling stopping in November) and a tile labelled "now" nine hours on. Two sources, neither the fix the first attempt tried (calming any LOOK card with no `next` or finding, which turned a freezer at 15 °F "usually 0 °F" all fine). (1) brAIn's own rise check read DATES as figures ("2 Nov compared with 1 Nov" is a rise of a half) and raised a fine card to worth a look. (2) The reply's `verdict` field said worth a look beside its own contract lead "Normal, because …". Before it, a quiet card could hold a last card that said worth a look or needs action.
- **A card that is not all fine says what to do, and a card about nothing is not a card** (#793). A house's cards reported non-events, admitted what they could not check, and led with what was ruled out. "Nothing new since <date>" is the rewrite that takes a fine verdict off, and it was drawn as a card. The one-short-gap-line rule (`trim_gaps`) was kept because `test_card_blind_spots` held a single gap line beside real news. The contract's Thin-data line lost "unless it changes what to do". `tests/test_card_verdict_next_step.py`.
- **A card about nothing happening does not take a card's place.** Reports was mostly "no cooling since 17 Oct" in heating season and "neither unit has run since". `tests/test_card_quiet_fold.py`, `measure-reports`.
- **An all-fine card nobody asked for is a name on the quiet line, and the design review reads Reports, not the store** (#1134, #1085). Folding needed "nothing changed" too, so a normal card with new figures stayed a card. The house's design review was handed `load_insights()` — every stored card, folded ones included, with no chip and no Next — and cut every field with `clip`, so it reported cards Reports did not show and lists "cut with …" that only its own cut had made. **Its findings are the rows on the list too** (#1409): it was handed `findings_store.listing()` whole — held and settled rows with their hold's reason under "what a look said" and no status — and reported brAIn's set-aside rows as cards listed beside the verdict that they should not be.
- **A blind spot is never a card's headline, and a card and a finding about one thing are one item.** A house's Reports carried "Climate air handler: no cooling in 10 days, nothing to compare", "both doors stayed shut" over "brAIn can't see a reopen after 1 AM", and a dehumidifier card at 0 kWh beside a finding with the same numbers. `tests/test_card_blind_spots.py`.

## Cards and findings

- **A card answers for its own rows on its next run.** A card's rows were listed under "do NOT report these again", so its next run could not say the data still showed one and nothing cleared it: rows stayed open long after the data stopped supporting them. The OTHER live rows came with their figures because a card and a finding about one freezer told one story with two numbers and two names. `tests/test_card_contract_words.py`.
- **A card and an open finding about the same thing say the same thing.** A card counted a plug's dropouts as 7 since Saturday beside a finding counting 14 in 7 days, and another said an interlock automation now handled a case the open finding still told the owner to fix. `tests/test_card_finding_figures.py`.
- **A card's headline decides whether it is a finding's story** (#759). An air handler card led with the coil sensor's stuck reading beside an open finding about the same sensor, and one passing mention of the outdoor temperature kept it a second item, because the twin rule asked that EVERY entity the card names be the finding's. `tests/test_card_one_mention.py`.
- **Two cards about one device or system tell one story.** A water card opened on the septic pump's figure beside the pump card's own different one, and a new heat card sat beside an older air-handler card still worded as live about the same heat pump. `tests/test_card_siblings.py`.
- **A report and a finding about the same thing link to each other.** They told one story twice with nothing between them.

## Live data

- **A card is written once, so a card about NOW is frozen at then.** Right for "last week's energy" and wrong for the ones people pin to a dashboard: a door that is open, a machine running, a room being held at a temperature all read as current and are not.
- **A live card has TWO ages, and the foot reported one of them** (`liveAgeText` / `paintLive` / `makeFrame(insight, live)`). The foot said `Updated 3 d ago`, once, for both, which is wrong in both directions at the same time: it invites somebody to distrust a reading that is current, and to trust a conclusion written three days ago against data that has since moved. So a card that declared `live` said **`Analysed`** — a claim about the prose, and nothing else — and the readings got their own line naming how many were live and when they last **arrived**. **Three states and they are three different claims**: `waiting` before the first fetch lands, the age after it, and `not updating` when a fetch fails. A failure kept the arrival stamp so the line did not reset to `waiting`, which would read as a card that has never been live. The pinned-run overlay applied to the expanded view too, and nothing had noticed it. `tests/manual/measure-cardlive.mjs` was verified against the old behaviour first: nineteen failures, including the pinned-run overlay. Its own first run found a flaw in itself — a thrown timeout abandoned every finding already gathered and reported only the missing helper — so every wait in it notes rather than throws.
- **House › Reports folded the foot to one claim** (`cardage`, `liveAgeText`): it says Updated N ago (or From <run> on a pinned run), and the live line renders only on a fault. That is the rule the note now states; the two-ages line above is what it replaced.

## The card on screen

- **A card leads with its answer, and the two things people want from one are named buttons.** Reported as *"cards are strange… they all say custom… they list the raw question which is of no value… there isn't an easy way to edit the card… no way to export it… charts don't use space correctly"*, and each half of that had one cause. The two named buttons were Refine and Share; Share has since moved into ⋯ and Refine is ⋯ › Edit. Without `_enqueue`'s reset, the next plain Regenerate was told last week's change and wrote it on the card as its reason. Regenerating an asked card used to re-ask the question and leave a second copy beside the first.
- **A card is as tall as what it holds.** Stretched to their row, a text-and-tiles report beside a chart carried 250–450px of blank above its foot and a question's Yes/No sat ~100px under the question; a page that drew nothing kept the frame's 320px (the size snippet never reported 0) and a one-line page was held at 120px.
- **Share has two halves and neither sees the chart's DOM.** The `/local` page's old 11px "analysed …" foot is gone; a frame sized to the chart's own content inside a fixed-ratio card was the inner scrollbar.
- **On an insight card the title gets the row, and two controls sit beside it.** The head used to be one flex line — icon, category, title and six `flex: none` icon buttons — so on a phone the buttons took ~250px of a 390px card, the category wrapped to three lines and the title truncated to `Upstair…`. For a while `⤢` stayed on the card as the only action on *what is on screen*, and Regenerate/Edit/Feedback/Add to dashboard/Delete lived in `⋯`. Refine and Share were named buttons that lost their words under 600px (`cardActionButton`, still defined in app.js, no longer called); `measure-cardhead` was made to fail on a title clamped at all after the first cut of the narrow head did that on a 1024px two-column grid.
- **There is no "refresh everything" control**: it was an unlabelled circular arrow in the top bar.
- **Since House › Reports the head is Ask · ⋯ and nothing else** (`cardask`, `cardMenuButton`): ⋯ was then Share, Past versions, Run, Delete, and Edit, Make recurring and the automation suggestions were links in the Ask dialog (`fillRefineMore`). Since then Ask became a conversation with brAIn about the report ("ask should become chat — talk about it with brAIn", the owner) and the dialog moved to ⋯ › Edit.
- **A card is drawn in the dashboard's look unless somebody asked for another** (#539). The owner: insight cards "don't go great with the Morph theming". The hue order came from a house's layer that overwrote this sheet in every card frame to get it, and the contract's quiet marks from the same house's card overrides. Before Morph's themes carried their own status colours, a framed card in a Morph theme fell back to the light sheet's.

## The ask bar and the prompt

- **Reports carries no ask box of its own** since the House redesign cut the second one; for a while a card's **Ask** dialog refined that card or carried the question into the chat (`seedAsk` filled `#chatInput`).
- **The ask bar's third verb ate its own questions, and the fix is one word further along.** The question went down the one-off path — spending a Claude run to produce a refusal card about an intent it could not arm, for something a card would have answered. A 409 on a Remove of an entry already gone left a card nothing could ever clear — nothing to splice, nothing to reload, nothing to put back.
- **A prompt nobody can see is a prompt nobody can argue with.** The Edit dialog's box was labelled *"Analysis focus (the prompt for this category)"* and it is one paragraph of a dozen blocks, every one shown nowhere, so a card that kept coming back wrong could only be argued with by guessing. That is issue #300. Storing the prompt that produced the card on screen was the obvious design and the wrong one. A textarea of the assembled text would have the card stop seeing new findings and go on citing a measurement from March with nothing on screen to say so. `get_category` in a preview is the same failure this route exists to end, one layer further in.

## Ideas, milestones and deep review

- **An idea costs nothing until it is taken, which is why it is a separate PAGE.** Onboarding studies a home once and proposes a card set, and that was the only moment brAIn ever offered an idea of its own — the ask bar and "＋ Make recurring" both start with somebody already knowing what to ask, so a house that has since grown a solar array and four months of history goes on generating whatever was chosen in its first twenty minutes. It was the Home group's Ideas tab at first. The empty page once said which of three silences it was — nobody has asked, the last look failed, or brAIn looked and had nothing to add (`house.py`'s rule one tab over); two were cut as prose nobody needed. `system_prompt`'s `%` broke on its own content, which its own test caught. `measure-ideas` gained its gap check after the first cut used `findline`, a class the stylesheet has no rule for, which rendered "⚡ ideasuggested 1 h ago" and which every other check in the file passed straight over.
- **It is a row now, not a page** (`#ideasRow` under House › Reports, `#viewIdeas` gone, `switchView("ideas")` lands on Reports): the row was its name and Run with Save · Ignore on each idea. It then moved to Memory (next bullet).
- **Deep review and Suggested reports are Memory's, as folded lines** (#539). The owner: "I have no idea why deep review and suggested reports are on insights … those seem more like memory items to me". The deep review was a row under House › Reports (`#kReview`, polled while Reports was in front) before this.
- **The moment a measurement first has an answer is the only moment it is news.** `house.py` is the smallest possible answer to the silence. Mixing milestones into the categories would put seven cards nobody chose onto a dashboard whose whole design is that you chose what is on it.

## The weekly report, the brief and their schedule

- **A weekly report is not a brief with a longer timer.** A model asked to pick while writing picks the row carrying the most detail rather than the most consequence. Skipping a week to protect an hour is the trade `weekly.due` deliberately does not make. "Nothing was learned" for an unreadable memory log would be the same lie the checks avoid with "I could not look".
- **The one thing to do this week is chosen across the week, and the rank it replaced is journaled beside it.** Three warnings about one hub never outranked anything, and a `serious` row that is a fact of the house topped every week.
- **The weekly report says where the measured energy went, and keeps what is not a lived-in room apart** (#1324).
- **A restart is not a new morning.** Both stamps were kept in memory only — so restarting the add-on set them to zero and the next window sent a second one, about the same morning or the same week. Restarting is the first thing anybody does after changing an option, which made that the ordinary case rather than the unlucky one.
