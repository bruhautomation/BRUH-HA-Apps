# brAIn: the Resident (first look and investigation)

Signals and the one queue, the first look and its gates and caps, investigations and cases, the safety lane, refine/hold, grounding, outcomes and reflect, worked examples.

Part of the project notes (see `CLAUDE.md` › *Where the notes are*). Read this file before changing what it covers; add a note here, not in `CLAUDE.md`.

- **History is a pane, and every row but a snooze can be deleted** (`#viewArchive`, `today.clear`, `POST /api/history/clear`). It was a closed `<details>` under Today whose rows carried only Restore, so it read as a pile nobody could prune, with no sentence saying what it was. It is Insights › History now: a heading and ONE line saying what the filter holds (`HIST_HINTS`, which also says Delete clears the record and not the answer — a paragraph under the heading said what History and Restore were, and the filter's line said it again), five counted pill filters with a hint each (the fifth, **Changes brAIn made**, is the next bullet's), a search box, **Restore** and a bin per row, and **Delete all shown**. **Delete clears the record and changes no decision** — the settled ledger is not touched (it is the dedup index, and dropping a key would re-raise what somebody answered), so `today.clear` records `{row id: stamp}` in the hidden-cards file and `history()` skips a group whose newest row is not newer than the stamp: the same problem answered again next month is a new answer and comes back. A snooze is not deletable — it returns on its own date whatever the record says. Ignored/done/aside rows are deletable; the row id is `<filter>|<normalised title>`.
- **A row nothing has looked at is not invisible** (`triage.SHOW_AFTER_S` / `waiting_too_long`). A row still `triaging` past 15 minutes — the gate holding the batch, a limit, a slow look — carries `waiting_look`, counts in `open_count` (store, cases, mirror) and is listed under Needs you as "Not looked at yet", so "Nothing waiting on you" can never be said over a serious unjudged row. It stays `triaging`, so the look still judges it; the mirror counts it but does not list it, so HA events and Repairs wait for a verdict. The one-hour `STALE_S` sweep is unchanged.
- **How many findings are waiting for a look is a fault row once it is old** (`server._waiting_rows` / `reports.WAITING_LOOK_*`). Failed looks leave rows on `triaging` or showing "Nothing finished looking at this one" and nothing said how many. The diagnostics payload computes `waiting_for_look` and its oldest age once, and `reports.faults` (pure over it) names the count past two rows and two hours. The retry is the minute sweep offering `unjudged_open` beside `awaiting_triage`; `test_resident_unjudged` drives a row left by a failed look back into the pending list. **And the row says why, in its own words** (`reports._why_looks_waited`): it sent a reader to "the usage limit above", and the usage-limit row stands down under 10% of the day's runs — twelve refused looks is two hours of a look every ten minutes, exactly this row's threshold, and the row pointed at nothing. It names the Resident's own refused and failed runs (`journal.summary`'s `by_source.resident`) and what the Resident last said (a gate holding its looks, `resident.last_error`); a day of looks over a house whose rows flap was driven end to end and every row was judged within a look or two, so a wait past two hours is looks refused or held, and that is what the row has to say.
- **A row's wait runs from when it was sent to the look, not from when it was filed** (`findings_store.waiting_since` / `_send_to_look` / `triaging_since`, `server._waited_s`). Three paths send an OLD row back to `triaging` — a held row re-reported with a new detail (`_absorb`), a severity rise (`refresh_details`), the startup re-look (`migrate_folds`) — and every clock read the filing `ts`: `stale_triaging` called it "left mid-look past the hour" on the next minute sweep, before any look was due, and showed it under `triage.UNJUDGED`; and `unjudged_open` re-offered silence-surfaced rows only within a day of the FILING, so it was never offered again. The fault sweep read "18 findings are still waiting for a look, oldest 328 h" on a day the first look ran 120 times (reports #82). The stamp is set wherever a row goes back to the look and dropped when a verdict lands (kept by `untriaged`, which is still waiting); `stale_triaging`, `triage.waiting_too_long` and the diagnostics' oldest age read it, and `unjudged_open` has no window on the filing — a row on screen saying nothing looked is offered until a look answers.
- **A row's look is reached in bounded time, and a row a look judged is not waiting for one** (`server._resident_absorb` / `RESIDENT_LOOKED_INTO`, `signals.batch(overdue_before=)`, `tests/test_resident_waiting.py`). Three ways a filed row went on "waiting for a look" for days on a house whose looks all succeeded. **The pending cap dropped filed rows to keep live copies**: a chatty protected entity is one hot signal per change, held raw until a look folds them, so between two timer looks (the hot quota spent) it put hundreds of copies of ONE signal on the list, and past `RESIDENT_QUEUE_MAX` the cap's only non-hot victims were the rows a rule filed — re-offered every minute and dropped by the same tick, least salient (the oldest) first, for ever. Over the cap the live signals are folded first (`signals.dedupe`, the look's own fold, repeats kept) and a filed row is never the victim while a live signal is left. **The reserve was filled by salience**, whose recency weight favours the newest row, so a row that missed one look missed every later one; `_offer_findings` carries `waiting_since` beside `finding_ts` and a filed row past `triage.SHOW_AFTER_S` goes first in the reserve, oldest waiting first. **A `watch`/`investigate` row stays `triaging` while its investigation runs** (the looked-into rule) and was counted by `_waiting_rows`; it is held in `RESIDENT_LOOKED_INTO` for exactly that window and not counted. It is not stamped on the row: a restart mid-investigation leaves it `triaging` and the next look judges it again.
- **A held verdict a later report contradicts is looked at again, and a fault filed before the cross-fold is one row** (`findings_store._absorb` / `migrate_folds`, `triage.LOOK_VERSION`). Four copies of "this window is wrong" stayed held as "zero cooling in late September is normal" while their own details showed hours of cooling the sensor missed; a later copy proved it and nothing went back to the verdicts. A re-report absorbed by either fold whose `detail` CHANGED sends a HELD host back to `triaging` (never over `elevated_by_person`, never a row a person ended — that row is not live), so the first look judges it again under the current claim rule. Rows held before the claim rule carry no `v` on their triage record and get ONE fresh look at startup, bounded to `MIGRATION_RETRIAGE_MAX` newest; the same one-time pass folds live model-written rows about one entity across producers (open beats held, newest detail wins, nothing settled, never the Resident or a fixed row). The marker is `/data/findings-migrations.json`, written even when nothing changed, so a restart is not a new pass.
- **Every producer files through `triage.gate`, and the first look is the one judge** (`triage.gate` / `findings_store.awaiting_triage` / `server._resident_pass`). The separate triage drain is gone: it had no caller but its own tests once the Resident's first look took the queue, and keeping a second judge with a second vocabulary (`elevated`/`held` against `ignore`/`watch`/`investigate`/`act`) was two answers to one question. `gate` still marks a row `triaging`, still drops a muted producer's rows, marks a row from a rule three Wrongs deep with that record (`record_hold`), which a silence then holds rather than shows (findings.md, `rule_record.holds` — the household's answers are the reply that said so), and still must not mutate what it is handed; silence still surfaces (`UNJUDGED` after `STALE_S`, `RUN_FAILED` for a look that died). An AST sweep in `tests/test_triage.py` still asserts no `findings_store.add_many` call in the panel is missing its `triage.gate`. What the drain's tests pinned that the Resident's did not — two ticks at once spend one look, memory reaches the look, a skipped or unreadable reply holds nothing — is driven on the Resident path in `tests/test_triage_retired.py`. `BRAIN_MODEL_HAIKU` now reads the first look's tier (`model_plan.env_exports`), which is the same tier.
- **"What you'd need to do" is written by the run that LOOKED, and the rule's sentence is only what the card shows until then** (`findings_store.refine` / `fix_by: resident` / the triage block's `wrote_fix`). A check writes one `fix` for every row of its kind — *check its power and its connection (batteries, Wi-Fi, the hub it pairs through), then reload its integration* under eleven different devices in one pass — because a pure function over a snapshot cannot say more than its rule knows, and a person holding the device called it generic and useless, which it is. The run that writes it is the Resident's investigation, which already reads the finding with Home Assistant's own tools, so the specific answer costs no new run (the retired triage drain did this first, and `record_triage` writes no `fix` now): `findings_store.refine` writes the case's sentence over the rule's on the row — **never** on a held row, which is shown to nobody, and never when the run wrote none, which leaves the card it always was. `refresh_details` does not touch `fix`, which is what stops the next pass's re-report putting the generic sentence back, and `wrote_fix` rides in the triage block so the payload says whose sentence it is. The generic one is not kept: it says nothing the check's own docs do not. Two rows in the same report were the add-on about itself — `dev.unavailable` filing *brAIn Usage Limits has been unavailable for more than a day* with battery advice, on the day the tracker was stuck — so `checks/devices.SELF_PLATFORMS` keeps the `brain` integration's entities out of every device check: the add-on's own faults are `health.py`'s to report, with the switch named, and a finding about them under somebody else's remedy is a bug report filed against the wrong thing. And `reports.faults` folds the checks a missing snapshot key took down into that key's own row (`_missing_snapshot_key`), and drops a store row that repeats the snapshot row's sentence — seven rows about a September too warm to fit a heat-loss model to is one fact, and every check is still named where the fault is.
- **Every signal is offered to ONE queue, and the queue is where nothing decides** (`panel/signals.py` / `server._resident_offer` / `_offer_findings`). Phase 1's argument is that a rule reading one instant cannot know whether its row matters, so every producer — a checks pass, an override, a sub-threshold baseline deviation, a reply, the morning window, a state change off the bus — becomes a signal with a **salience that orders and never decides**, and the only thing that decides is a model that has seen the batch, the memory excerpt and the open cases together. `SIGNAL_KEYS` is closed on purpose (a signal carrying a device class would be a rule smuggled back in), so a findings row rides its `finding_ts` beside the published keys and `_offer_findings` is the one door for one, deduped by row id because `run_checks` and the queue sweep can both see the same row. `_FINDING_ADAPTERS` routes a check's row to the adapter whose kind weights fit it (`check:climate.*`→`from_thermal`, `check:chore.*`→`from_appliance`, `check:base.*`→`from_baseline`), and `_measurement_signals` reads the baseline store and states the pass already fetched — **never a new fetch inside the attention loop**, which is also why "hot safety" is decided from `SAFETY_SUBJECTS`, filled by the bus's `on_event` at the moment the event is admitted, rather than by re-reading a state.
- **The first look is cheap by contract and the gates HOLD the batch rather than spending it** (`server._resident_pass` / `_resident_gate` / `resident.parse_first_look`). Haiku answers `ignore | watch | investigate | act` per signal on `RESIDENT_LOOK_S` (600s) or within `MIN_LOOK_SPACING_S` of a hot one, and `NEVER_IGNORE` floors — a protected entity, a safety device, a hot signal — override the verdict rather than trusting it. No credential, `auto_enabled` off or a spent budget leaves the batch waiting with nothing spawned: `triage.STALE_S` is the bound and the stale sweep is what makes a batch nobody looked at visible, `triage.UNJUDGED`'s rule carried forward. The stale and `awaiting_triage` sweeps run on a **60s cadence** inside a 5s tick, because two full store loads twelve times a minute on an SD card is not what the tick is for and hot signals arrive on the queue, not through a file. `RESIDENT_STATE["running"]` is flipped synchronously (`start_auth_check`'s rule) and the per-day cap now counts first looks. A failed run surfaces every filed row with `RUN_FAILED` and re-queues the rest — silence surfaces, one step earlier than triage did.
- **An investigation reads and a case is what it writes; `act` files and notifies and never calls a service** (`server._resident_investigate` / `_resident_apply` / `findings_store.add_case`). Sonnet runs `run_analyst` — reading tools only — with `CASE_SCHEMA`, at most `MAX_INVESTIGATIONS_PER_LOOK` (3) per look and only while `LEDGER.allows` the tier, surplus re-queued and counted; `parse_case` is handed `read_entities` (the signal's subject and evidence plus every id the transcript mentions) so a claim about an entity the run never looked at is demoted rather than believed. A hot safety `act` files a `problem` case at `critical`/`stakes: high` with one `notify` action carrying `consent: False`, through `add_case` and `_announce_findings`; every other `act` is an investigation. `test_resident_loop` asserts the analyst stub was never reached on that path, because "nothing on this path calls a service" is a claim about the process and not about the prompt. `Ledger` is per tier per local day: the cheap tier never stops on it (only on the usage tracker), and `ESCALATE_JOB` steps up only when the ledger allows, else the case stands and its `detail` says it was not escalated.
- **A filed row the first look thinks is worth more than nothing is looked into BEFORE anybody is asked** (`server._resident_apply`'s `looked_into` / `_settle_looked_into`, `RESIDENT_STATE["resolved"]`). *"Claude is turning up a ton of findings it can answer itself"*: a `watch` or `investigate` (or a non-hot `act`) on a check's row elevated it to the person first and investigated it second, if the day's allowance reached it at all, so the card was read in its rule's generic words and the answer arrived after the question. Such a row now stays `triaging` and its investigation decides how it ends, read off the STORE afterwards rather than off the investigation's return: `refine` moved it to `open` (shown and announced in the words of the run that looked), `hold_after_look` held it (announced to nobody — the self-resolution — and still in History › Set aside by brAIn), or it is still `triaging` because the run failed, made no claim, met a duplicate or was PARKED, and it is then shown with the look's reason exactly as before. **A spent allowance is a reason to wait, never to hide a row**: a parked one is shown now and refined in place later (`REFINABLE` includes `open`). Only a row that was still `triaging` when the look answered is held back (`statuses` read before the investigations), so a row already on screen never vanishes into a run. Safety signals, a fallback verdict (nobody decided) and a hot `act` are shown straight away as before. The investigation's contract says the half the person was carrying: a `question` is for intent, preference or a physical fact no sensor records, a `fix` never tells somebody to check what a tool can read, and `dismiss` names the commonest honest endings (cleared, a passing moment, normal for what it is). A `fix` never tells somebody to read a log, a trace or a file brAIn can read with its tools either (the Supervisor log about brAIn's own error was one), and the first look's `ignore` names a question the house already answers. `SONNET_PER_DAY` doubled (6/16/32) and `MAX_INVESTIGATIONS_PER_LOOK` is 3, because an investigation that sets a row aside is a card nobody reads; the usage budget still gates every run. The outcome log now carries the investigation's verdict beside each watched row's look, which is why `test_outcomes` counts eight rows for four looks.
- **"Nothing finished looking at this one" is a state a look can still end, and filed rows are not starved of looks** (`findings_store.late_verdict_welcome` / `unjudged_open`, `signals.batch(filed_reserve=)`, `triage.HOT_LOOKS_PER_DAY`). A real house had twelve of fourteen open findings carrying `triage.UNJUDGED`. Silence surfacing is right and stays; what was wrong is that it was permanent and reached in steady state. Four causes, each reproduced in `tests/test_resident_unjudged.py` before the fix. **A late verdict was dropped**: `record_triage` touched `triaging` rows only, so a row the stale sweep (or a failed look, `RUN_FAILED`) had shown kept "nothing looked" for life even when a look answered about it minutes later; an `untriaged` row on `open`, never one a person put back, now takes the look's verdict record — the reason replaces the sentence — while its STATUS stays `open` whatever the verdict, `ignore` included, because a row already on screen never vanishes into a run. Such a row is written and not RETURNED by `record_triage`, which returns only rows it moved out of `triaging` — the list every caller announces — so a late verdict, and a look that keeps failing, never ring the phone about a row already announced; an `untriaged` verdict on a row already marked `untriaged` is no change at all. `_resident_apply` writes the look's reason onto such a row before its investigation refines it, `_resident_run_failed` re-queues the filed rows with the rest, and the minute sweep offers `unjudged_open` beside `awaiting_triage` so a restart cannot strand one. **The batch was hot-first**, and every change to a protected entity is hot, so a busy house filled every slot; half the batch (`MAX_BATCH // 2`) is now kept for filed rows, which carry a deadline a live signal does not, and only a TRIPPED safety sensor goes ahead of them; past the reserve, filed rows still precede live cool signals. **The queue cap could drop a filed row**; it takes a live one first. **Hot looks a minute apart could spend `MAX_PER_DAY` by mid-morning**, and the share meant to stop it did not: 150 hot looks plus a look every ten minutes is past 200 by late morning, so every row filed after that waited until midnight with every look that ran succeeding (reports #82, five fixes that each moved a symptom). A hot pull-forward now spends only looks the timer will not need before midnight (`triage.hot_look_allowed` over `timer_looks_left`, `server._seconds_left_today`), so the cap stands only against a runaway; past that only a tripped safety sensor pulls a look forward, and a day-cap hold says so in `last_error`. `tests/test_resident_two_days.py` drives two simulated days (150 chatty entities, a pass every 6 h, real caps, the stores on the simulated clock) and asserts every filed row a verdict within two looks.
- **A tripped safety detector is filed by code, not by a verdict** (`server._note_safety` → `_safety_trip` / `_safety_clear`, `SAFETY_TRIPS`, `SAFETY_STATE`). The only route from a leak to a phone used to be a Haiku look that ran (credential, `auto_enabled`, budget, day cap), parsed and answered exactly `act` — and the never-ignore floor matched words in the signal's KIND, which for a bus signal is `state`, so the real floor was `watch`; the case it filed sat under `resident`, which had no urgency entry, so quiet hours held it until morning; and its text was a fixed sentence, so the second leak on a sensor deduped against the first for ever. The lane runs off the bus's raw hook on a TRANSITION into `signals.HOT_SAFETY_STATES` (a sensor already `on` at boot is not a trip — the look's `_file_safety_case` is the second door for that), schedules a task on the pump's own loop (held in `_SAFETY_TASKS` so the loop cannot collect it), and asks no gate: a credential, the switch and the budget exist to stop brAIn SPENDING and this spends nothing. **One case per trip**: the text carries the local date and time, which is what makes each trip its own row against a ledger that dedupes by text; `SAFETY_DEBOUNCE_S` folds a chattering detector into one. Source `safety`, which `notify_router.PRODUCER_URGENCY` reads as `now`; `tier_of` also escalates `critical` + `stakes: high` for the Resident's own rows — and `SEVERITY_BY_STAKES` still tops out at `serious`, so an investigation's confidence cannot reach a phone at 3am, only something deterministic can. No notify target means `persistent_notification.create`, because "nobody configured a phone" is not a reason a leak is reported to nobody. A clear **annotates and stops the ladder** (`findings_store.annotate`, `notify_router.stop_escalation`) and never clears the card: water that dried still came from somewhere. `eventbus._admits` lets a tripped detector through the hard ceiling, because a flood is exactly when its one event must not be the one dropped. The model only enriches: a look's `act` on a lane trip becomes an investigation that refines the lane's card. `tests/test_safety_lane.py` drives `_note_safety` inside a running loop into the REAL `_announce_findings` during quiet hours with every gate closed.
- **The deeper look corrects the cheaper one, and the row it was sent to look at is its subject** (`_resident_investigate(refines=)` / `findings_store.refine` / `hold_after_look` / `resident.parse_dismissal`). The investigation was told the row it was investigating was "already in front of the homeowner, do not claim it", so it abstained (a paid run with no trace) or filed a differently worded sibling. Now the refined row is shown as its subject and kept OUT of the open-cases list, its answer rewrites everything a card SHOWS — claim, detail, fix (`fix_by: resident`), evidence, stakes — and **never `text`**, the key a check re-reports under; severity only rises. A reply with an empty claim and `dismiss` holds the row with its reason (never over `elevated_by_person`), and a run that failed or came back empty leaves the row where it was. `_open_case_rows(exclude=)` is how the row stays off the forbidden list.
- **A finding is judged on its claim, and the look is shown the evidence it rests on** (`resident.CLAIM_RULE` / `DISMISS_RULE` / `signals.FILED_DETAIL_CHARS`). A real house had "this sensor reads 0" rows held as "zero cooling in late September is normal for the season" while each row's own detail said the air conditioning ran for hours that day — a reason about one number, answering a claim about the pair. Two causes, both fixed. The prompts never said what was being judged, so `CLAIM_RULE` is one string in both tiers' system prompts: judge the claim against every piece of evidence it states, a disagreement between two readings IS the claim, a reason that explains one of the numbers does not answer it, and no reason may contradict the row's own evidence; `DISMISS_RULE` sits beside the row an investigation may dismiss. And the first look could not SEE the detail: a filed row's title and sentence already filled `ROW_CHARS`, and the detail rides as evidence after them, so it was cut on exactly the rows a rule filed — a row carrying `finding_ts` gets `FILED_DETAIL_CHARS` more. There is deliberately no word gate on the dismissal's reason: prose is what gets reworded, and a gate over it is a name gate.
- **An open card never carries a look's argument for not showing it** (`findings_store.shown_look` / `_names_another_entity`). A late `ignore` (stored `held`) about a row silence already showed keeps the row `open` and its verdict on the record, and its reason — "the known dropout, nothing new" — is not shown beside it on the tab or the feed (a row a person put back still shows the reason they overruled); holding the row would break "a row on screen never vanishes into a run". A reason naming an entity that is neither the row's own nor in its evidence was written about another row and is not shown either. **A reason saying the row repeats another open finding is the `duplicate` word written as prose** (`triage.says_duplicate`): `parse_first_look` reads it as `already: duplicate`, so the row is set aside rather than shown under "…is already an open problem for you", and `house_reason` drops such a sentence and `shown_look` a reason that contains the row's own text (`_names_itself`) — a shown reason is what hides a check's own fix (`shown_fix`), so a reason about the list took the card's what-to-do with it (#448). **And a look that says the problem is over, or is just the thing's ordinary state, says so in a word too** (`resident.ALREADY` gained `recovered` and `explained`, `tests/test_resident_already_recovered.py`): "the speaker has been switched off since the evening" and "all the plugs are connected again" were `watch` reasons beside cards that stayed on the list asking somebody to act (#537). Either word sets the row aside the way the other three do — a fresh row is held, an untouched shown one too — and a safety floor or a person's touch still wins. **And an investigation that finds the house already holds the answer says so in a word too** (`resident.CASE_ANSWERED`, the case contract's `already`, `resident.unfinished`'s `already_known`, `tests/test_already_known_is_not_asked.py`, #697): a run that gives it is held like `nothing_needed` — a refined row with its detail (where the answer is) as the reason, a new one not filed with the detail written `explained` on the decision trail, a `question` case included. **Never over a floor**: `unfinished(case, floor)` takes `never_ignore`'s floor for the signal, and with one set (a leak, smoke, a protected lock) or with `stakes` high the word is not read and the case is filed without it — a tripped detector is not a thing the house "already answers", whatever a run says, the floor the first look holds over its own `already` words. Never a reading of a look's reason, a step or a detail: the first fix was a regex over sentences with "already" in them, and it held "the automation already locks the door at night, so the lock motor needs replacing" and "you already said it is fine, but the battery is now at 4%" — a problem that mentions what the house knows reads exactly like a conclusion that nothing is new, and no clause rule tells them apart, so every such sentence is a test that must still file. The word is the look's `answered` for the reason the other `ALREADY` words are words.
- **A look's count may not disagree with its detail** (`findings_store.quotes_other_counts`, in `shown_look`, #443). A check refreshes a row's numbers every pass (`refresh_details`) and the look's reason keeps the old run's: "49 drops" beside a detail saying 36. A reason that counts something the detail counts, with a number the detail does not carry, is not shown until a look writes one about the row as it is — dropped rather than sent back, because sending every refreshed row back would spend a look per pass.
- **A look's reason that only repeats the row is not a conclusion** (`findings_store.echoes` / `without_echo`). `refine` wrote the investigation's claim as the triage reason, so a Resident card read "brAIn checked: <its own title>" with no conclusion ever written. `refine` keeps the reason a look already gave (never an echo, never `UNJUDGED`/`RUN_FAILED`), `record_triage` and `hold_after_look` write no echo, and the tab (`listing`) and the feed (`cases._from_finding`) blank an echo on read, which covers rows already stored and `add_case`'s own stamp (its claim as the verdict that wrote it, pinned by `test_cases`).
- **Grounding reads the tool traffic, never the answer** (`conversations.tool_traffic` / `_entities_read`). `parse_case`'s invented-evidence guard compared evidence against entity ids in the CLI's final reply — on a CLI that echoes its JSON every cited id was "read", and on one answering with `structured_output` and a sentence no neighbour ever was, so real cases were refused and logged as "made no claim". It reads the run's own JSONL in the engine store (tool_use inputs and tool_result content only, assistant text excluded) and answers None for a transcript it cannot read, which checks nothing — "I could not look" must not throw an investigation away. A refusal is logged as a refusal (`RESIDENT_STATE["refused"]`), distinct from no claim.
- **"It did not run" is grounded in the automation that runs it, in code** (`resident.drivers_of` / `ungrounded_did_not_run`, `server._DRIVEN_BY`, the gate after `parse_case` and the escalation in `_resident_investigate`). A case said the basement dehumidifier "hasn't run" while the house was damp, from its state history and whichever humidity reading the run found, while the automation that drives it triggers on one other sensor. The controlled-by lines in the prompt (`_CONTROLLED_BY`) already named that automation and a run skipped them, so the check is a gate: each checks pass builds `{actuator: [{alias, reads}]}` from the snapshot's automation configs (`shadow.would_do` for what each acts on; `House.entity_refs` over its triggers and conditions for what it reads), and a case whose words say a device did not run (`_DID_NOT_RUN_RE`: hasn't run, didn't turn on, should have run, stayed off…) that names a driven device and a reading beside it (`READING_DOMAINS`, from its evidence, its entity or its words), none of which any of that device's automations reads, is refused whole — `refused` on the verdict log, the automations and their readings as an `explained` line on the decision trail. A device nothing drives, a claim with no reading in it, and a reading that is a trigger OR a condition all pass; an empty map (before the first checks pass) gates nothing. **And idle is the rule working while what starts it is not due** (#558, `resident.not_due` over `shadow.bounds_of`/`waiting`, `server._LIVE_READINGS`/`_DRIVEN_ALIASES`): a dehumidifier was called idle or broken from its plug's power while the one sensor its automation starts it on sat under the threshold.
