## Stores, floors and the nightly pass

- **A nightly measurement nobody can ask for is a fix nobody can check** (`h_baselines_run`, `POST /api/baselines/run`). Its control is in ⚙ → Diagnostics beside the rows it is about. The route **starts** the pass rather than awaiting it — a month of hourly statistics over every numeric sensor is minutes of recorder work, longer than ingress will hold a request open — and the outcome is read back off `/api/baselines`. The start guard is a second flag (`starting`) flipped **synchronously**, for `start_auth_check`'s reason: `create_task` only schedules, so two presses in one tick would both pass a guard their own task has not set yet; `build_baselines` owns `running` and is also called by the loop, and one function setting another's guard is how two callers stop agreeing about what is in flight.
- **A floor's cost is silence, and silence is indistinguishable from broken** (`panel/house.py`). Every floor exists because a confident answer over data that holds none is worse than none, but a fresh install then says nothing for weeks. So every store answers one question about itself in one shape: `progress()` → state, `have`/`need` in a named unit, a `reason`, a `ready_at` and one sentence true in that state.
  - **A store's floor lives in the store**: `progress()` is a method on the module that owns `MIN_DAYS`/`MIN_SAMPLES`/`MIN_BUCKETS`, never a number restated in `house.py`, because a second copy of a floor drifts invisibly.
  - **`ready_at`'s arithmetic is here and its RATE is the store's**: only the store knows whether its unit is a nightly pass (a day), an hour of watching, or a weekday (five in seven, which is why the weekday rhythm takes two weeks and the weekend one five). One division, so two tabs cannot disagree.
  - **"I could not look", "there is nothing here to measure" and "not enough days yet" are three different answers**: a refused build and a house with no outdoor thermometer are both `unavailable` with the *store's own* sentence and **no date**, never `collecting`; `stale` is the fourth and the only one worth fixing. A store that raises is `unavailable` naming the exception rather than taking the payload down.
  - `energy` has no store, so its fetch (and the hour-long cache) belongs to the caller and `progress()` stays pure; it has deliberately no `stale`, being computed from the recorder each time.
- **`end_time` has no default, and the argument that is NOT there cost two features** (`ha_data.history_params`). Core's `/history/period/<start>` without `end_time` answers with **start plus one day**, not up to now, and an empty `[]` then reads as "nothing here" rather than "I could not look". The parameter is positional and required: a caller that wants Core's default writes the date that produces it, because `None` would be an invisible spelling nobody can grep for. The WebSocket `recorder/statistics_during_period` has no such default. Tested against a real aiohttp server, because a fake that accepts `params` and discards them cannot see a missing parameter.
- **A cap is spent AFTER the eligibility filter, and what it cut is said** (`baselines.select` / `BASELINE_STATE_CLASSES` / `BACKGROUND_CATEGORIES`).
  - **The fix.** A `total`/`total_increasing` meter has no `mean` statistic and `checks/baseline.eligible` drops diagnostic and config entities; the filter is the readers' own rule moved ahead of the cap, and a test holds the two sets equal.
    - The nightly pass fetches the registries once, before the baselines.
    - A registry that did not answer costs the category filter only, and the store says `categories_read: false`.
    - `eligible`, `cut_count` and a sample of the cut ride the store, `progress()`, `/api/baselines` and `/api/diagnostics`.
  - **The unit floor.** `MIN_ABSOLUTE_MOVE` is per unit (`checks/baseline.min_move`, °C still 0.5), shared with `forecast.decline`.
  - **The wording.** The whole-history fallback's detail counts hourly readings, not "weeks".

## Baselines and drifts

- **"Unusual" is a word until something measures it** (`panel/baselines.py`). Per numeric entity, what it normally reads **at this hour of this day of the week** and how far it normally strays, from a month of long-term statistics, nightly, for no Claude turn.
  - **The bucket is an hour of the week in the HOUSE's timezone** (from the cache `run.sh` writes); UTC smears a morning across two buckets and moves it twice a year. An unreadable or unknown zone falls back to UTC and *says which*.
  - **Spread is a MAD, not a standard deviation** — one spike sets a σ wide enough that nothing falls outside the band (BRight's p99-not-max).
  - **A reading that never moves has no spread, and dividing by it makes every change infinite**, so `spread_floor` is a fraction of the reading's size *and* an absolute, and an entity whose whole history is one value is `flat` (no baseline).
  - **A bucket under `MIN_SAMPLES` says nothing**, and `deviation` falls back to the whole history *labelled* `source: "overall"`.
  - **And nothing here decides anything** — it answers "how far outside its own normal, in its own spreads"; the checks and the model decide what is worth saying.
  - `candidates` sorts before capping so the cap takes the *same* entities every night. Built by a nightly loop and only ever *read* by the checks snapshot, which must not spend minutes of statistics queries every six hours on an answer that changes over weeks.
- **A drift is invisible to the band, structurally, and the number says how much** (`baselines.trend` + `forecast.decline`). `base.unusual` buckets by hour of the week, so a drift moves the bucket along with the reading. The line is fitted to the hourly means minus each hour-of-week median (which removes the daily and weekly shape and leaves a slope), and graded against the spread **about that line**, the one estimate the drift cannot inflate.
  - Two floors: **both halves of the window must agree on a direction** — that tells a drift from a V (a season turning) and a step (a meter replaced). A move smaller than its own noise is not a move.
  - And **five thermometers drifting together is the weather rather than a device**: the whole `device_class` stands down. Past a handful of rows it says nothing (the `base.unusual` cap).
  - **The `state_class` gate is the one that would otherwise have fired in every house**: a `total_increasing` meter always has a slope. The gate is in `build`, because a trend nothing should read is a trend nothing should store, and both `base.unusual` and the trend require `measurement`.
  - A reading far outside its band on a drifting sensor is reported once, as the drift: they share `baselines.trend` and one `eligible()` (as `dev.unavailable` and `dev.zwave_dead` do).
  - **And a drift with an outside cause is not a device going wrong** (`forecasts.outside_cause`): an outdoor class (`OUTSIDE_CLASSES`: weather, soil moisture, money, distance), a unit per kWh, a weather/grid/phone platform, a device that also has a `device_tracker`, a name that cannot mean indoors, or an area called outdoors. It is an exclusion list on purpose, because an allow-list by class would silence unclassed readings the check should fire on.
  - One least-squares fit: `baselines.least_squares`.
- **A drift is the season until last year says otherwise** (`baselines.fetch_year_ago` / `year_ago_move`, the trend's `year_ago`, `forecast.same_weeks_last_year`). The nightly pass reads the same weeks one year earlier (one `day` statistics call, only for sensors with a consistent trend) and stores how far they moved. The check is quiet with no figure, with fewer than `YEAR_MIN_DAYS` daily rows, and when last year's weeks moved the same way at least `SEASON_SHARE` as far. Each is a `drift` line on the trail; a filed row says what last year's weeks did.

## Appliances and chores

- **Nothing in Home Assistant says a cycle ended, and every rule on a smart plug is a wattage somebody typed** (`panel/appliances.py` + `chore.waiting`). The numbers are measured per machine from ten days of its own five-minute statistics.
  - A power reading is **bimodal**; a sensor without that shape gets **no profile** rather than a guessed threshold. `MIN_SPAN_W` and `MIN_SPAN_RATIO` must both hold (a ratio alone passes a phone charger, a span alone passes anything large).
  - **The floor is a low percentile, never the minimum** — one zero in a power cut would set the idle level for good.
  - **"Below the threshold" is not "finished"**: a dishwasher's dry phase draws almost nothing. The gaps between draws are themselves bimodal, and **the widest jump in that sorted list is the appliance saying how long its own quiet phases last**. `MIN_DRAWS` counts draws, not cycles: two gaps is the fewest a jump can be found in.
  - **A blip is not a cycle** (`MIN_RUN_MIN`).
  - **"unloaded" cannot be seen from power at all** — there are three states and the fourth is a person pressing something (To-do and notification buttons); inferring it would invent the one fact the chore is about.
  - The check's floors: `QUIET_MIN` on top of the measured settle, `STALE_HOURS`, `MAX_ROWS`, and **the name gate** — narrowed to a washer, a dryer and a dishwasher by the sensor's name, longest phrase first; the one guess in the file, made where being wrong is cheap. Urgency is `whenever` so quiet hours can hold it.
  - What each machine is doing *now* is the one measurement the checks pass fetches for itself, cheap by construction: a handful of profiled sensors over fourteen hours.
- **A device whose integration SAYS its job ended is the same chore read off a different signal** (`checks/chores.job_done`, `chore.job_done`, `snapshot._job_history`). It reads a **transition**, never a word: a `sensor` whose live state is in `JOB_FINISHED` is a *candidate* (`job_candidates`, pure, shared by collector and check), and a chore only when the recorder shows a `JOB_RUNNING` word before that finish, walked newest first through further finished words and the `JOB_PASS_THROUGH` ones; anything else breaks the chain. Walking back through every finished word keeps a restart from making an old print look new. Same `QUIET_MIN`, `STALE_HOURS`, `MAX_ROWS` and `house.should_report`.
  - The printer is `PRINTER_WORDS` on the status entity's own name (never bare "printer"), and **not** in `WAITING_KINDS`; a washer with both a profiled plug and a status is the power path's (`_power_owns`).
  - The fetch takes only the candidates, at most `JOB_FETCH_MAX`, over `STALE_HOURS` + 1h, and **no request at all** when nothing says finished; unreadable history raises `CouldNotLook`, filed as skipped, so it clears nothing.
  - **And a tick cannot be contradicted by it** (`todo_store.UNSEEN_DONE_SOURCES`): the status says `finish` until the next job, so re-running after Done must not bring the chore back. `chore.waiting` has the same shape and was deliberately left as it was.
- **And power cannot say a drum is full** (`chores.FINISHED_TEXT` / `FULL_TEXT` / `_door_since`, `findings_store.add_many`'s `was`). The row says "… finished a cycle and nothing has run since" (the clock time in the detail), and says still full only where a door or opening sensor on the same machine has stayed shut since the cycle ended; a door that has moved files nothing. A reworded check text is a new dedupe key, so the row carries the old wording as `was` and `add_many` drops one whose `was` is a suppressing settled key.
- **An appliance's busy level is read off its RUNNING readings, never off the whole window** (`appliances.noise_floor` / `BUSY_PCT` / `select`).
  - A high percentile of the whole window is the idle level for an ordinary washer, so the busy level is the median of readings above both bimodality floors at once (`MIN_SPAN_W` above idle and `MIN_SPAN_RATIO` times it); the median, so a heater phase does not lift the threshold over the motor phase.
  - The 40-sensor cap ranks sensors `chores.kind_of` names first, then by id. The store records `eligible`, `cut_count` and a sample of the cut, because a washer cut before profiling is `chore.waiting` silent with nothing to say why.

## The thermal model

- **Every climate question is the same two numbers about a room, and brAIn held neither** (`panel/thermal.py` + `checks/thermal.py`). Newton's `k` (loss per hour; its reciprocal is the time constant) and `h` (gain in degrees per hour), measured per room from a month of hourly statistics against **one** outdoor reference, and **the reference is recorded in the payload**.
  - **The sun is the confounder and night is the gate**, in `build` rather than the check (a measurement wrong in every house is one nothing should store).
  - **A fit is not a measurement until it is graded**: the delta must span something, and the slope is graded against the scatter about its own line.
  - **`device_class: temperature` is carried by readings that are not a temperature anybody stands in** (`thermal.DERIVED_WORDS` / `is_derived`: dew point, feels-like, wet bulb, heat index, frost point). The refusal is checked **before** the outdoor words ("Outdoor Dew Point" satisfies both) and in `room_candidates` too. A name gate, made where wrong is cheap: refusing a real sensor costs the model and **the payload says it found no reference**.
  - **A number outside physics is not a room** (`MIN_TAU_H`/`MAX_TAU_H`), **indoors and outdoors have to agree what a degree is** (a Fahrenheit reference against a Celsius room is wrong by 1.8), and **a freezer carries `device_class: temperature` too** — a room spends the month inside a band people live in.
  - `hours_to_warm` returns `None` for a target above the room's ceiling: "it cannot get there" is not "it takes a long time".
  - **And nothing here decides anything**, the split `baselines.py` and `closures.py` keep.
  - `climate.underheated` needs the arithmetic **and the evidence** (the room never once seen at what it is asked for), because a thermostat stopping at its setpoint hides the room's ceiling; it says nothing when the month held no cold night. `climate.heat_loss` needs four measured rooms and the room fast in absolute terms too. They share `underheated_rooms` so one room is one card. Both `whenever`.
  - **Three more read the model against what a room is doing now, or against every morning.** `climate.preheat` needs `rhythm` (wake time), `baselines` (what the room reads then) and the model (how long the climb takes) to agree, **weekday mornings only**, and says nothing until the wake time is *measured*. **The proof the heating does arrive is required**: a room still short two hours later is `climate.underheated`'s.
  - `climate.window` fires on a fall more than twice what the room's own `k` allows; its allowance is read where the fall **started** (`test_the_allowance_is_read_where_the_fall_STARTED`). **And a fall that ENDS below the reference is refused**: a cooling room approaches the outdoor reading and cannot pass it, so crossing it means outdoors, refrigeration, deliberate cooling or a wrong reference.
  - `climate.freeze` asks when the room reaches the floor water in an outside wall is at risk at, only about a room **already falling**.
  - The live checks read **five-minute** statistics (`thermal.recent`, fetched by the checks pass as `appliances` does), since an hourly mean cannot see a window opened forty minutes ago. `window` and `freeze` are `now` and may break quiet hours; `preheat` is `whenever`; they stand down for each other's room, freeze first.
- **A probe on a heating or cooling machine is not a room, by name or by what it does** (`thermal.NOT_ROOM_AIR_WORDS` / `equipment_jumps`). The words cover the refrigerant line, the flue, the burner and the rest of the machine (whole words), and a reading whose largest rise across one or two hourly means passes `EQUIPMENT_JUMP_C` on most days at nearly the same clock hour is left out with that sentence in `unmeasured` — no room warms that fast and the weather does not run on a timer. The area is then measured from its ordinary air sensor or not at all. Pure over the hourly means the pass already fetched.
- **A heat-loss ranking compares the rooms something heats, and nothing else** (`checks/thermal.conditioned_areas`). A room counts as heated when it holds a `climate` entity (whatever it is doing now) or a `switch`/`input_boolean` named heater, radiator, underfloor or baseboard; a room the household said is unheated, uninsulated or unconditioned is not. That note is read off the facts store (`unconditioned_from` → `snap["unconditioned"]`): an `area:` subject names the room, an entity subject names its room through the registry. Unheated rooms are neither outliers nor in the median nor counted towards `MIN_ROOMS`, and the trail carries one `explained` line each. A house whose thermostats are not in Home Assistant gets no heat-loss ranking.
- **The outdoor reference is RANKED, the ranking is shown, and a person can overrule it** (`thermal.rank_outdoor` / `choose_outdoor` / `daily_swing` / `settings_store.thermal_outdoor`).
  - Each candidate carries sentences:
    - an area not called outdoors rules it out;
    - an outdoor-named area or no area counts for it;
    - a strong or weak outdoor name counts for it;
    - the weather entity's own integration counts for it;
    - reading what the weather entity reads counts for it. This is the one signal that needs no word, so it works in any language.
    - a coil, pool or pipe name, or reading far from the weather entity, counts against it.
  - The nightly build reads the leading candidates' month and lets the daily swing decide, because air swings and a probe in a wall does not.
  - The setting overrides the ranking. A choice that is not a thermometer here is said and ignored, never silently swapped.
  - The store carries `outdoor_source` / `outdoor_why` / candidates, and the drill-down renders them.
  - A confident `world_model` reading of `measures` replaces the NAME's evidence in `rank_outdoor` (outdoor +2, machine −3, room air −2, derived excluded) and never the area, integration, weather or swing evidence.

## Rhythm and habits

- **A schedule is a timer; a rhythm is measured** (`panel/rhythm.py`). Of the action miner's causes, **the first one caused by a `person` is the house waking up** — not motion (a cat, the heating), not a light (an automation at dawn). Two numbers a day are kept, not a timeline.
  - Floors: `MIN_DAYS` before it answers; **weekdays and weekends measured apart** (a weekend takes about five weeks to exist, which is the floor doing its job); `MAX_SPREAD_MIN`, because a house that stirs anywhere between 05:00 and 11:00 has no wake time; and a day already recorded is **widened, never replaced**.
  - **And the median is CIRCULAR**: settle times either side of midnight (four inside forty minutes of it) have a straight median of **12:00**, so `circular_spread` takes the short way round. `tests/test_rhythm.py` asserts the failure against `statistics.median`.
- **A night belongs to the day it started in** (`rhythm.DAY_START_MIN` / `house_day` / `PERSON_CAUSES`).
  - **The fix.** A house day runs 04:00–04:00, fixed on purpose so a night is never refiled as the store ages. `first`/`last` are taken in that day's order and stored as clock minutes, so no reader changed (`brief.due`, `signals.night_window`, `healing`'s settle+60, `evening.left_open`, `climate.preheat`).
    - `settle_minute` reads the house day: 00:30 on Saturday is Friday night's settle.
    - `wake_minute` reads the calendar day: the wake still to come.
  - **Repair of old stores.** A store with no `day_start` marker is repaired once on read. The two rows each post-midnight night was split across are dropped; rows the new rule would write identically are kept.
  - **`voice` is a person** (an Assist command is the most deliberate thing a person does to a house). `unattributed` still is not.
- **One habits arithmetic behind three ledgers** (`panel/habits.py`). `habits.grade` is the one implementation of days, share of possible days, circular median, occupied band and still-happening; `routines.py`, `override_ledger.py` and `manual_ledger.py` keep their files, floors and vocabulary and delegate (`routines._grade`, `override_ledger.pattern`, `manual_ledger.shape` / `off_pattern`). `habit_lookup.habit_of(entity_id)` reads all three for `/api/habits` and the `habits` tool, in its own module because a module the ledgers import may not import them back. The clock arithmetic is `panel/circular.py`, a leaf importing nothing of the panel's (its median is the standard library's, since `baselines.median` would close an import ring through `baselines → house`), and `rhythm` re-exports it under every existing name.

## World model and situation

- **A device's role is read once per fingerprint and checked in code, and below the floor the word list still answers** (`panel/world_model.py` / `attribute` / `view`).
  - **Why.** `_HOT_WORDS`, `WAITING_KINDS`, `OUTDOOR`/`DERIVED_WORDS`, `WATER_WORDS`, `NIGHT_WORDS` and the rechargeable guess were all English name gates. A house that names its stopcock `hoofdkraan` had no shutoff in its leak playbook. Its dew point became the outdoor reference unless it happened to contain "dew".
  - **The reading.** The `entity_model` job (Haiku, low effort, tool-less, `--json-schema`) reads about 60 candidates a call. A candidate is an entity some word list asks about: temperature/power/battery sensors, binary sensors, switches, valves and lights. Rows are numbered and names are fenced as data.
  - **What the reply may hold.** Every field is a closed vocabulary validated in `parse`. A word outside it is `unknown`, never the nearest match. A field outside its domain (`APPLIES`) is forced to its default. `space` must be one of the house's own areas.
  - **Re-reading.** The store is keyed by a fingerprint over name, manufacturer, model, platform, area, unit and device class. `view` serves only rows whose fingerprint still matches, so a renamed entity falls back to the word list until it is read again rather than wearing its old name's role.
  - **Rows the reply skipped.** They are stored at confidence 0, so they are not paid for every night. They still read as no answer.
  - **What a reader gets.** `attribute(world, eid, field, fallback)` returns the fallback (a value, or a callable so the word match is paid only when needed) when there is no row, the row is under `CONFIDENCE_FLOOR`, or the value is `unknown`. Every call site keeps its old list as that fallback, so an empty or unreadable store is byte-for-byte the old behaviour. The snapshot carries it as `snap["world"]`, `{}` on any failure.
  - **When it runs.** It rides the nightly baseline loop behind `_resident_gate`. It stops on the first failed batch with a backoff (`RETRY_BASE_S` doubling to a day), saves after every batch so an interruption loses one batch at most, and caps itself at `MAX_BATCHES_PER_PASS`.
  - **Where it may widen a check.** It may widen a check whose cost of being wrong is one finding: a nozzle read as a heater is not "implausible". That is the same direction-of-cheapness trade `measures_something_hot` already wrote down.
- **The house's mode is a guess about a frame, and every guess is checked against the frame** (`panel/situation.py`).
  - **The frame.** `build_frame` is deterministic, from one REST `/states` read per refresh (`REFRESH_S`, 180 s) plus the closures/appliances stores and the world view. It never fetches history. It holds presence (person.*, or GPS device trackers only when there is no person entity), rooms with recent motion, media or lights, closures open against their `usual_open` share, running appliances, the weather, opted-in calendar titles and occasions.
  - **When the model is asked.** The `situation` Haiku turn runs only when the material `fingerprint` changes (the part of the day, who is home, rooms, unusual closures, running appliances, the weather condition, the calendar, occasions — never the minute), at most every `MIN_RUN_SPACING_S`, under `MAX_RUNS_PER_DAY`, and behind `_resident_gate`.
  - **`parse` refuses, in code:**
    - a mode outside the closed set (it becomes `unknown`);
    - `away` without presence data, or with anybody home;
    - rooms that are not the frame's;
    - "unusual together" items that cite no frame key;
    - a sentence with a number or an entity id not in the frame (`grounded`), or with body, health or sleep words (`_BODY_RE`). That sentence is replaced by the previous one marked `sentence_stale`.
  - **What `reading` serves:**
    - `unknown` for a missing, failed or stale frame (`FRAME_STALE_S`), never `away`;
    - the deterministic `presence_mode` with `source: "presence"` when the stored answer was about a different fingerprint, because an answer about an older house is not served as current.
  - **Safety.** The mode is context in the first look's prompt and nowhere else. A wrong `away` cannot lower a never-ignore floor, which `TestAWrongModeNeverSilencesSafety` drives through the real Resident loop.
- **`sensor.brain_house` is aged by its file, and its rule lives in a module with no HA imports** (`custom_components/brain/house_state.py`).
  - The panel mirrors the reading to `/config/.brain/situation.json`. This is skipped when `/config`'s parent is missing, `publish_state`'s rule.
  - The reader judges freshness by the file's **mtime** against the `stale_after_s` the file itself carries. That is `BrainHealthSensor`'s rule, and for its reason: a corrected clock must not keep a stamp from the future fresh.
  - A missing, garbage or stale file is `unknown` with a `reason` attribute, never unavailable.
  - The reader is pure so the test drives it without a Home Assistant install. The entity is driven separately through permissive stubs that restore `sys.modules`.
- **A bus frozen at boot is held to the fallback for ever** (`eventbus.EventBus(rhythm_payload=, known_ids=)`). `rhythm.py` needs a fortnight before it has a measured night and the registry changes whenever somebody adds a device, so both are **callables** resolved on the existing `CTX_REFRESH_S` timer; a dict still works, and a raising callable keeps what it had rather than blanking the window. `resident` is a claimed `run_sources` source (`store: "engine"`) in both halves, or a case's "see what it looked at" opens nothing.

## Tools over the measurements

- **Every measurement is a tool, over the panel's own routes** (`ha_mcp_server.what_is_normal` / `room_physics` / `appliance_status` / `house_rhythm` / `door_habits` / `habits` / `simulate_automation` / `recall`). A run with a tool asks, where a run handed a block hopes the block covers the question. Each reads the route the Knowledge tab reads (`_panel_get` over loopback, `explain_change`'s arrangement), so there is one implementation of each answer, and a store that has not measured yet says so **in a sentence** — `room_physics` names the rooms it does know. `simulate_automation` is `_replay_config` plus `trials.evaluate` behind `POST /api/simulate`, days clamped to `SIMULATE_MAX_DAYS`, a refusal whole (`shadow`'s rule). All eight are on `ANALYST_TOOLS`. Tests drive them with `_panel_get` answering the shape its route really sends, never a guessed key.
- **`explain_change` and `get_activity` call the panel over loopback rather than re-implementing attribution.** Same reasoning as `brain findings` going through the API instead of the store files: one implementation, one answer. Both are read-only and on `ANALYST_TOOLS`, so an unattended card can ask what caused something; a panel that is not up is reported as such rather than as a quiet house.
- **Traces are read live and keyed by config id** (`get_automation_trace` over `trace/list` + `trace/get`).
  - `.storage/trace.saved_traces` is written only at shutdown, and Core keys a trace by `<domain>.<unique_id>`: a timestamp for a UI-made automation, never its entity id.
  - `get_automations` returns the `id`.
  - `get_automation_config` uses the WebSocket `automation/config` / `script/config` by entity id, which reaches YAML and package automations. The REST editor endpoint knows only automations.yaml. This matters because unattended runs have no file access.
  - The panel's trace checks share the keying and row-shape bugs. They are WP5's, not fixed here.

## Energy

- **Which meters is the half that produces a number nobody can tell is wrong** (`panel/energy.py`). Summing every `device_class: energy` sensor double-counts (clamp, inverter and plugs all carry it). The set is **Home Assistant's own** (`energy/get_prefs`, grid `flow_from` only); no energy configuration is a sentence rather than a guessed group.
  - **Cost only where a cost STATISTIC exists** — guessing HA's derived cost-sensor id quotes another sensor's number.
  - **Both windows are seven complete local days**, ending at midnight, never `now`; the day count is the **fewest any one meter gave**, not the average.
  - **A negative day is a meter reset, not a negative amount of electricity**: dropped, which shortens the window and stands the comparison down.
  - Consumption is asked of the recorder (`types: ["change"]`) where it can answer, with the cumulative `sum` as fallback; deriving from `sum` costs the first row, so the fetch reaches a day further back than the report.

## The action miner

- **A state carries no cause, and the logbook is the only place Home Assistant records one** (`panel/actions.py`). The miner reads the logbook, walks each event's context chain, and files every change under a cause from a closed vocabulary (`CAUSES`, shared by the check, the tab's filter and the MCP tools).
  - **Proximate and root cause are different and both are recorded**: an automation started from the UI carries a `context_entity_id` *and* a `context_user_id`.
  - **`context_domain` is not the runner** — it is `light` for a plain `light.turn_on`, so the authority is the context *entity's* domain and only then the three domains that run something.
  - **A change with no context is `unattributed`, in as many words**: a wall switch and a device's own integration reach Core identically.
  - **`when` is an ISO string over REST and a float over the WebSocket stream**, out of the same processor; handle both.
  - **Nothing is persisted** — every function is pure over one fetch of a window.
- **brAIn knows what brAIn did because it wrote it down, not because it can tell.** Its REST calls are indistinguishable in the context chain from any integration's. `ha_mcp_server.record_action` appends every service call to `/config/.brain/actions.jsonl` at the `call_service` chokepoint `protected_entities` is enforced at, and `actions.read_ledger` joins the logbook against it inside `BRAIN_MATCH_S`. An **area or device target is recorded and deliberately not resolved** — a wrong expansion would attribute somebody else's change to brAIn. Writer and reader are **different processes running as different users and neither can import the other**, so `tests/test_actions.py` drives the real writer into the real reader.

## Activity and the Timeline

- **The Activity tab is the fourth kind of knowledge, and it has no badge.** Memory is what is *true*, a finding what is *broken*, a hypothesis what brAIn might have *wrong*, and this is what *happened*: a record, not a queue, so the one badge stays Findings'. Fetched per visit and never cached. The overrides ride above the list because they are evidence rather than history. The cause **moves to its own line** on a phone rather than being deleted; an unattributed row shows nothing in the cause slot and the foot says once how many there were; no evidence block over an empty window. `tests/manual/measure-activity.mjs` drives the real `renderActivity`.
  - **An unreadable logbook says so in plain words and hides the room and cause filters** (the sentence names no file or check, only that the Logbook should be turned on in Home Assistant).
  - **House › What happened** opens on the situation sentence (`#actNow`, off `/api/situation`; no sentence, no line; since #1303 the heading and the line describe one moment, see below); the tab's intro and each group's blurb are cut (the server still sends `blurb`); the cause chips are one **Caused by** `select.sel` with no counts; the paragraph's press reads **Ask**. Earlier / Later stay.
- **A logbook with a cause column is still a logbook, and nobody thinks in state changes** (`panel/episodes.py`). People think in **episodes**: the TV on in the lounge from eight until eleven, somebody home at 17:40, the hall sensor nine times between two and three.
  - **A reading is not an event** — `READINGS` is dropped whole and **counted**, and the foot says what it left out; a `button` is in that set because its state is a timestamp.
  - **An episode is a run, and a gap only ends one that had already STOPPED**: a subject's own `gap_s` merges a burst, and an episode whose last state is **active** is extended across any gap. `duration_s` has one meaning in both states (`open` is carried, never inferred).
  - **The sort is by SUBJECT and not by cause**; the matcher is domain-first, the device class consulted only where the domain cannot answer. An unclassed `binary_sensor` goes to *Everything else* (`closures.is_closure`'s refusal); an unclassed **cover** may fall through. `reads` (`span`/`count`/`moment`) is on the subject table, because a momentary sensor's span is not a duration.
  - **Nothing here asks a model** (`curiosity.py`'s rule; a test scans the module for the imports), because it runs on every visit.
  - **`away_spans` refuses more than it claims**: the roster of people is taken FIRST and a span cannot open until every one has been seen; a `device_tracker` is a phone, not a person; an ongoing span ends at `now`; a window in which nobody moved says nothing.
  - The **paragraph** is the only part that spends and it is a press (`h_activity_summary`, cached against the window, `brief.py`'s gather-then-one-sentence split; its contract forbids writing about the person rather than the house).
  - Layout: the row must **wrap** rather than ellipsise the automation's name, and `.actlist` needs `grid-template-columns: minmax(0, 1fr)` because an implicit `auto` column grows to one `nowrap` cause.
- **Activity agrees with the house NOW, and names what explains a burst** (`episodes.group(live=)` / `actions.lifecycle` / `server._house_now`). An ongoing episode whose last state differs from the live state ends at the live `last_changed` and the live state becomes its own row (`from_live`); only a window that ends now is touched. Home Assistant's own start/stop lines (which `mine` drops) become a "Home Assistant restarted" row, and uncaused rows near it carry `near_restart`. Six or more uncaused changes to one state in one second are ONE row naming the integration only when every entity shares it; a change with a recorded cause is never collapsed. People is `person.*`, and a `device_tracker` only when the house has no person entity and only a GPS/mobile_app one. The current window is labelled "Now" and one paged back to by its dates.
- **The Timeline is narrowed on the server, before a block is capped** (`server._activity_narrow`, `area`/`kind`/`q` on `/api/activity`). A room (off `_NAMES`, `-` for none), a kind of thing (an `episodes` subject) and words in a device's name; the room and kind facets are counted before those filters, and `away` is read over the whole window first because an empty house is about people, not the lounge. The window is 6, 12, 24 or 48 hours (`ACTIVITY_MAX_HOURS`), its select on the Earlier/Later row sized to its words (`.actnav #actHours`). The label between the buttons (`actDayLabel`) says **Now** for the current window, the window's own dates for one paged back to; `measure-activity` holds both and the row at 390.
- **The Timeline's heading says who is home and matches the line under it, and a device that kept flipping is one row** (#1303; `actHeadline` / `actStandsOut` in app.js, `episodes._fold_repeats`, `situation.SYSTEM`). The heading names who is home from the frame's person entities (a count past three; asleep, waking, guests and away say themselves), a stale sentence is not shown, and the line under it is what stands out now from the frame (rooms in use, machines running, by name, no reading). Rows of one entity with the same first and last state, every one finished and shorter than its section's gap, each starting within `REPEAT_JOIN_S` (30 min) of the one before, fold into one `kind: "repeat"` row ("Plug flag · on 5 times · 6:45–8:26 PM") after the section's one-off rows: a fold is a burst, never a day. Locks & safety and Doors & windows (`REPEAT_NEVER`), a person's rows, a count section's, a burst, a restart, a row still going and one read off the house now never fold. The situation prompt asks for a sentence that agrees with the mode and carries no reading unless it is the news.
- **A restart is one line, and the Timeline opens on the house** (#1121, `episodes._fold_dropouts`, `SUBJECTS` order). Dropout bursts (unavailable/unknown, nothing caused them) within `DROPOUT_JOIN_S` of each other are one event: folded into the logbook's restart row when there is one ("Home Assistant restarted at 9:04 PM", with "N devices went unavailable and came back" and the devices behind a tap), or merged into one collapsed row that claims no restart nobody recorded. Each device's own return (`_is_return`: an unattributed short run out of the dropout states, within `DROPOUT_JOIN_S` of the burst) folds with it. "And came back" is said only when every device is back, read **per device** (#1140): its live state when the window ends now, else its own last logbook state and any return of its own, never the burst's last state (`_mass_changes` keeps each member's last state in `_last`, uncapped, and `group` strips it before the payload). Otherwise the line says how many are still offline (`offline`) and the tap lists those first (`_offline_first`); a lone burst row carries the same count. Membership is never read off the capped `entities` list. `unknown` is where scenes and buttons rest after any restart, so it is not "not back". A lone device's blip folds only into a burst or a restart, and one still unavailable never folds. The Home Assistant section is last. The empty-house band is a sentence ("The house was empty from … to …").
- **The Timeline says what happened in household words** (`episodes.said_words` / `EVENT_WORDS`, `situation._machine_name`, `renderAwayBand`). A classed `binary_sensor`'s on/off is said in its class's words (`said_first`/`said_last`: "reported a problem → back to normal") and the row is called by its name without HA's appended class label (`called`: "Dryer"), beside `name`/`first`/`last`, which filters and grouping keep reading; the paragraph's prompt gets the same words. A power meter running is the machine ("Dryer is running"). When the house was empty is a quiet time marker at the head of the list, one line a day ("Today the house was empty from 10:05 to 12:18."), never a box beside the line about now.
- **`/api/activity` still answers with rows when a caller asks by `limit`** (`server._activity_rows`). The MCP `get_activity` tool reads `total` and `actions` and always sends `limit`; the Timeline never does. A request carrying `limit` gets the mined rows newest first, narrowed by `cause`, capped at 1000 (default 200), with `overrides`; one without carries none, as the tab expects.
